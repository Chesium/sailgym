# Shrink the horizon before hunting for tacks

Two published agents have learned to tack upwind from scratch with no demonstrations: Bink's SAC on a simulated and then robotic Optimist, driven by rudder-rate and sheet-rate style actions, and Suda and Nikovski's PPO and SAC on a kinematic polar-diagram boat. Both made far fewer decisions per manoeuvre than SailGym does. Bink acted every 0.5 s and MERL every 60 s, and Bink also paid a dense, level-based distance reward and a −1,000 timeout penalty. Nobody has reported tacking emerging at sub-second decisions with ±10 terminal rewards and potential-based shaping, and no sailing paper ablates decision rate, discount or reward type, so Task U's negative result is new rather than contradicted. Read together with this project's own logs, the literature points to three compounding obstacles. First, a tack spans 40–160 decisions inside a 4,000-decision effective horizon, where white action noise averages out and TD errors compound. Second, SAC's soft critic carries an entropy charge of about 8,000·α; at the α values U actually reached, that charge outweighs the whole 20-point gap between finishing and failing, so the critic ranks episodes by length rather than outcome. Third, the dense reward separating good beating from loitering is about 0.0014 per decision. The cheapest, best-supported moves are to act in 0.5 s blocks with γ rescaled to 0.99975¹⁰ ≈ 0.9975 (which puts U in Bink's regime), drop the entropy term from the critic (SACLite) and explore with temporally correlated noise. After those come reset-based discovery methods, reverse-start curricula and Go-Explore, which use the same simulator access the true-model planner already relies on; on the planner side, the 2 s value tail failed because it was open-loop, and every working value-tail planner refits V on its own visited states. One correction to the brief: the codebase runs U at **20 Hz with 3,600 decisions per episode**, not 10 Hz with 1,800, and all arithmetic below uses 20 Hz.

## Two agents learned to tack from scratch, both on far coarser clocks

**The closest precedent is Bink's 2024 TU Delft/MARIN thesis, and in several ways it is SailGym's twin.** Its learner was Stable-Baselines3 SAC with RL Zoo hyperparameters and a 2×64 tanh MLP, the same width and activation as SailGym's critic. Its continuous actions, "move rudder", "let sheet out/in" and "move weight", are rate-style actuation close to U's rudder-rate and sheet-rate pair, and it was trained from scratch, with no imitation data, to reach a target about 20 m upwind ([Bink 2024](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:1284613a-d9e7-4076-b120-8349acdca4ca)). Beating emerged unaided: "the agent learns an effective policy after about 400 episodes already… Finally it learns to tack to go upwind." The author credits γ = 0.998 with making tacking the highest-return strategy, and in-training success reached **95.8–98.2% across four validation runs after 400,000 steps**, about 4,000–5,300 episodes ([Bink 2024](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:1284613a-d9e7-4076-b120-8349acdca4ca)).

The setup differs from U in four ways that the rest of this report argues matter. The agent acted **every 0.5 s** (Δt = 0.01 s with 49 skipped steps). Every step paid a dense reward tied to the remaining upwind gap, plus actuator-rate and gybe penalties. Terminal rewards ran into the thousands: +2,000 for the target, −3,000 for capsize or leaving bounds, and **−1,000 for running out of time**. Start position, heading, speed and wind were all randomized. The result was also fragile. One of four seeds never tacked and instead learned to **scull**, pumping the rudder "to propel itself into the wind". In the test basin, two identically trained agents split: SAC_RI_3 succeeded 12/12 while SAC_RI_4 managed 3/6 because it drifted after tacks. Agents trained with observation noise often "did not demonstrate the ability to tack" ([Bink 2024](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:1284613a-d9e7-4076-b120-8349acdca4ca)). A 2026 journal version reports success comparable to conventional controllers plus roll tacking; only its abstract was readable ([Bink, Weymouth & Duz 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0952197626020889)).

**Suda and Nikovski's IJCNN 2022 paper is the second precedent**: continuous PPO and SAC learn to tack toward a mark 5 nautical miles upwind under a pure −1-per-step reward ([Suda & Nikovski 2022](https://merl.com/publications/docs/TR2022-102.pdf)). Its boat is a point mass whose speed comes instantly from a polar diagram, its action is a heading change, and it decides once a minute, so a tack costs only the minutes spent turning and none of the momentum loss that makes a SailGym tack expensive. Even so, **"SAC fails to even round the mark once for the first 2,000 episodes,"** while PPO improved almost at once; continuous PPO eventually beat a certainty-equivalence MPC in 87.4% of 1,000 paired wind realizations ([Suda & Nikovski 2022](https://merl.com/publications/docs/TR2022-102.pdf)).

The rest of the sailing record is thin. SB3-default PPO and SAC in gym-sailing learn rudder-only policies that "tack only once" ([gym-sailing](https://github.com/Gabo-Tor/gym-sailing)). A DockYard engineer credits most of a year's gains to redesigning the environment rather than the algorithm ([DockYard](https://dockyard.com/blog/2024/04/23/reinforcement-learning-a-year-with-the-sailboat-problem)). The Emirates Team New Zealand bot learned AC75 control by "trial and error" but discloses no algorithm, reward or curriculum ([Anyscale](https://www.anyscale.com/blog/sailing-to-victory-with-reinforcement-learning)). Most other work hand-designs the hard part: tabular Q-learning removes no-go headings from the action set ([da Silva Junior et al. 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7146235/)), and MERL's own discrete variant, like the stochastic-DP work it builds on, reduces a tack to a tack/stay macro-action with a fixed time penalty ([Suda & Nikovski 2022](https://merl.com/publications/docs/TR2022-102.pdf)).

**Successful analogues outside sailing, where agents learned to accept a short-term loss under a directional constraint, share Bink's recipe.** Loon's stratospheric balloons decided **every 3 minutes**, used distributional QR-DQN on 100 parallel actors, and softened a sparse "on-station" reward because "softening the transition at the boundary results in improved controllers". The learned controller held station 55.1% of the time against 40.5% for the hand-coded StationSeeker, a controller described as "effectively tacking towards its destination" ([Bellemare et al. 2020](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-020-2939-8)). Simulated thermal soaring used a local-in-time reward from vertical wind velocity and acceleration ([Reddy et al. 2016](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1606075113)), and cross-country soaring separated "leave the thermal" from "exploit the thermal" with a hierarchy ([Notter et al. 2023](https://www.github.com/ifrunistuttgart/RL_CrossCountrySoaring)). The shared pattern is a decision interval at the manoeuvre's own time scale, dense physically meaningful rewards, heavy randomization, and either large scale or hierarchy. None used demonstrations.

What has *not* been published is just as informative. No paper reports end-to-end tacking at sub-second decisions with small terminals and potential-based shaping, no sailing study isolates decision rate, γ or reward type, and none tries RL with demonstrations. U's result is therefore a new data point, and a controlled ablation along those axes would itself be novel. Several full texts were paywalled (the 2026 journal paper, a 2025 *Journal of Field Robotics* review and the DA-PPG curriculum paper), so a missed precedent cannot be ruled out.

## U hides a 100-decision manoeuvre inside a 4,000-decision horizon

**The brief understates U's decision count by half.** The physics step is 5 ms and a decision falls every 10 steps, which makes U a **20 Hz** task with **3,600 decisions** in its 180 s budget ([M0 report](../docs/ese3990/M0_report.md); [tasks.json](../experiments/ese3990/configs/tasks.json)); that is also why the planner's H = 40 and H = 160 are the 2 s and 8 s horizons ([M4U report](../docs/ese3990/M4U_report.md)). At γ = 0.99975 the effective horizon 1/(1−γ) is **4,000 decisions, or 200 s**, and γ^3600 ≈ 0.41, so the discount barely truncates an episode. A tack whose payoff arrives 2–8 s later spans **40–160 decisions**.

Set against the table above, the striking fact is that Bink's 250 s horizon and U's 200 s horizon are similar in seconds, and U's 4M-decision budget, 200,000 simulated seconds, *equals* Bink's 400,000 steps at 0.5 s. **U is not short of training time or horizon in physical terms; it cuts every second into ten times as many decisions.** Theory says that matters. Kalyanakrishnan et al. show that frame-skipping shrinks the horizon from 1/(1−γ) to 1/(1−γ^d), find that RL on Atari "deliver[s] substantially better policies when run with d > 1—in fact with d even as high as 180", and bound the "price of inertia" that repetition costs ([Kalyanakrishnan et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.03718)). Tallec, Blier and Ollivier show that DQN and DDPG "collapse with small time steps" because the advantage that ranks actions shrinks with the decision interval, so that "Q-learning does not exist in continuous time"; their fix rescales the discount as γ^δt ([Tallec et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.09732)). A 2025 study calls the horizon "the main cause behind the poor scaling of offline RL", with Q errors growing with distance to the goal and n-step SAC+BC reaching 88% where 1-step reached 44%, though that evidence is offline and goal-conditioned ([Park et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04168)). Practitioners rarely go near U's γ: TD-MPC2 caps it at 0.995 with action repeat 2 ([Hansen et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16828)), DreamerV3 uses 0.997 ([Hafner et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04104)), and SAC at γ = 0.999 did worse than at 0.99 on 5 of 6 MuJoCo tasks in RVI-SAC's experiments ([Hisaki & Ono 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.01972)). **U's γ = 0.99975 at 20 Hz sits above every continuous-control setting found.**

The project's own runs already contain the controls that make this concrete. M1 recorded that white Gaussian rate noise "resampled at 20 Hz, mostly averages out in the actuators" ([M1 report](../docs/ese3990/M1_report.md)); by inference, the mean of white noise over a 5 s window has a tenth of its per-step standard deviation, so a coherent multi-second rudder hold almost never happens by chance. The planner holds each action for 0.5 s and still finishes every held-out episode at H = 160, in a mean of **48 s** ([M4U results](../docs/ese3990/M4U_results.json)), which is direct evidence that 0.5 s holds cost U little; 0.5 s is also exactly Bink's decision interval. And crossing head to wind is not what PPO lacks: selected PPO from scratch tacks in **40 of 150** held-out test episodes, yet its mean furthest upwind progress is 8.2 m of 40 and it never finishes ([M4U report](../docs/ese3990/M4U_report.md)). The missing unit is the ~48 s beat, one or two tacks strung together at a VMG near 0.83 m/s.

**The dense signal that should guide that beat is about one-thousandth of a point per decision.** The time cost is 0.05/30 ≈ 0.0017 per decision, the potential −d/30 m pays about 0.0014 per decision at the planner's VMG, and the shaping available across the whole 40 m beat totals about 1.33, or 7% of the gap between finishing and failing ([tasks.json](../experiments/ese3990/configs/tasks.json); [sailing.py](../experiments/ese3990/sailing.py)). By inference, perfect beating and sitting still differ by ~10⁻³ per decision. Potential-based shaping preserves the optimal policy by construction, which is precisely why it cannot make loitering unattractive: any loitering path telescopes to nearly the same total. Bink's level-based upwind-gap term and −1,000 timeout did make loitering costly, but Bink also warned that intermediate rewards "should not be too large", because they encourage agents "to keep the episode alive" ([Bink 2024](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:1284613a-d9e7-4076-b120-8349acdca4ca)), and HER's authors found that distance-shaped (though not potential-based) rewards broke both DDPG and DDPG+HER ([Andrychowicz et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.01495)). Reward redesign changes the task, so it belongs low on the list.

## SAC's critic ranks episodes by length, not by outcome

**The literature names this failure "reward inflation".** SAC's entropy bonus enters the critic as a per-step reward before termination but never after it, so with the usual negative target entropy it makes the policy pessimistic and drives earlier termination. Yu, Zhang and Xu propose SACLite, which drops the entropy reward from policy evaluation but keeps entropy in the actor, and SACZero, which subtracts the reward's moving average; across 26 tasks SACLite was "comparable or even better", and much better on FetchPush and FetchPickAndPlace. The paper is a preprint tested only at γ ≤ 0.99 ([Yu et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12434)). Discriminator-Actor-Critic states the general rule that, with zero-valued terminal states, a per-step reward of fixed sign biases the agent toward survival or toward early termination ([Kostrikov et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.02925)). RVI-SAC removes the issue by turning terminations into resets with a learned cost ([Hisaki & Ono 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.01972)), and the 2025 preprint TECRL learns a separate entropy critic ([Zhan et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.11592)). **No paper tests any of these at γ ≥ 0.999.**

Applying the arithmetic to U, which is inference rather than a tested result, makes the problem stark. U's target entropy is −2 ([train.py](../experiments/ese3990/train.py)), so the soft value is roughly the task value minus **8,000·α**. Timeouts are bootstrapped rather than terminal ([sac.py](../experiments/ese3990/sac.py); [tasks.json](../experiments/ese3990/configs/tasks.json)), so only a finish or a failure stops the entropy charge. A finish after 50 s, close to the planner's 48 s, still pays 2α(1−γ^1000)/(1−γ) ≈ **1,770·α**. SAC ended U with α = 0.018–0.096 ([M4X report](../docs/ese3990/M4X_report.md)), which makes that charge **32 to 170 points**, against a 20-point gap between finishing (+10) and failing (−10). **The break-even is α ≈ 0.011.** Above it, the objective SAC actually optimizes prefers failing now to finishing in 50 s: how long an episode lasts outweighs how it ends.

**The offset is not the whole story, because the critic also misses its own fixed point.** In M4X's X1b arm (SAC with LayerNorm, from scratch), mean Q drifted to −5,700…−6,800 while the entropy offset was only −233…−728; under SAC with demonstrations, mean Q rose to +743…+3,314, far above the feasible maximum discounted task return of +15.7 ([M4X report](../docs/ese3990/M4X_report.md)). LayerNorm could not help for two reasons. The critic-normalization literature (RLPD, unit-ball normalization, the "high variance" study) targets high update-to-data overestimation, out-of-distribution extrapolation and early numerical blow-ups, all at γ ≈ 0.99, not the γ/(1−γ) size of a correct soft value ([Ball et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02948); [Hussing et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.05996); [Bjorck et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11222)). And, as M4X explains after the fact, a tanh critic's hidden features are already bounded, so LayerNorm bounds nothing new at the output ([M4X report](../docs/ese3990/M4X_report.md)).

**The project's data also show that "forgetting" with demonstrations is not a memory problem.** Every SAC-with-demonstrations minibatch is half demonstrations, drawn from a buffer that is never overwritten ([train.py](../experiments/ese3990/train.py)), yet seed 0 fell from 7/20 validation finishes at 1M decisions to 0 by 2M ([M4X report](../docs/ese3990/M4X_report.md)). With successes permanently in replay and Q sitting thousands above any feasible return, the decay most plausibly comes from critic overestimation, the out-of-distribution pattern RLPD's LayerNorm was built against and which did not transfer to this tanh network, rather than from washed-out successes. That inference has a practical consequence: self-imitation and success buffers will not help until the critic is fixed.

The remedies rank by how directly they hit this setup. SACLite is the cheapest: the critic learns only the bounded task value and α tuning stays in the actor ([Yu et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12434)). Average-reward reformulations and reward centering come next; centering's gains "increase further as the discount factor approaches one" ([Naik et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09999); [Hisaki & Ono 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.01972)). A lower training γ, with the shaping's γ matched, is justified by work showing a smaller planning horizon regularizes learning from limited data ([Jiang et al. 2015](https://www.ifaamas.org/Proceedings/aamas2015/aamas/p1181.pdf); [Amit et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.02040)). Scale tools come last, once the offset is gone: value transforms made γ = 0.999 stable on Atari ([Pohlen et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.11593)), and PopArt normalizes targets across magnitudes ([van Hasselt et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.07714)). SimbaV2 deliberately avoids centering because shifting rewards "can alter the optimal policy in episodic tasks" ([Lee et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.15280)); here, though, the policy change is the removal of an entropy-induced termination bias, which is the point. PPO has no entropy term in its value target, so its obstacles are horizon and signal, not this offset.

## Exploration has to commit for seconds, or start near the gate

**Temporally correlated action noise is the cheapest intervention with the most direct evidence on tasks shaped like U.** Hollenstein et al. studied MountainCar with an action cost, which has a "do nothing" local optimum structurally like U's timeout. Purely random Gaussian and Ornstein–Uhlenbeck policies earned similar returns there, yet inside DDPG, TD3 and SAC "learning either fails (Gaussian) or leads to success (Ornstein-Uhlenbeck)"; noise scale mattered most, and decaying it helped ([Hollenstein et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03787)). For MPO and SAC, pink noise (power ∝ 1/f) beat white and OU noise across the environments tested and is the recommended default ([Eberhard et al. 2023](https://openreview.net/forum?id=hQ9V5QN27eS)). For PPO, β = 0.5 is the best general default, but the best per-task β was 1.25 on MountainCar and 2.0 on a point-mass maze, where white noise lost significantly ([Hollenstein et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11091)). Two cautions are inference. Noise on *rates* integrates twice, from rate to rudder angle to yaw, so red noise can swing the heading far enough to end episodes, the "mines" failure mode reported for persistent ε-greedy exploration ([Dabney et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.01782)). And Bink's sculling seed warns that a 20 Hz rudder-rate action can find propulsion exploits that out-earn tacking ([Bink 2024](https://repository.tudelft.nl/record/uuid:1284613a-d9e7-4076-b120-8349acdca4ca)).

**Learned persistence keeps 20 Hz authority while committing for seconds**, which matters because the project plan forbids silently lengthening action holds. TAAC adds a closed-loop act-or-repeat choice to SAC and scored 0.96 normalized against 0.78 for the best baseline across 14 continuous tasks; persistent exploration alone scored 0.92, and naive fixed repetition was the strongest baseline ([Yu et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06521)). TempoRL learned up to an order of magnitude faster, but plain fixed repetition *slowed* DDPG on Pendulum ([Biedenkapp et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.05262)), so fixed repeat is not a free win everywhere. Q-chunking gives temporally coherent exploration but assumes offline prior data ([Li et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.07969)).

**Novelty bonuses work on classic sparse control but rank below noise and resets here.** VIME and hash-based counts solved sparse MountainCar, CartPole swing-up and HalfCheetah ([Houthooft et al. 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.09674); [Tang et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.04717)), and MaxInfoRL specifically repairs SAC's failure under action costs and local optima ([Sukhija et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12098)). Against that, a controlled benchmark found bonuses give "little to no performance gain" at a fixed budget and hurt easy games ([Taïga et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02388)). By inference, U's low-dimensional state makes counts cheap, but novelty in a 200 × 180 m box rewards reaching across the wind, not tacking.

**Resets are the strongest lever, and the planner already uses them**: CEM-MPC branches the live simulator, so restoring saved state is available. A reverse curriculum needs only arbitrary resets and one goal state; it grows starts outward from the goal by short random rollouts and keeps those where success is intermediate ([Florensa et al. 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.05300); [Florensa et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.06366)). The backward algorithm, which replays starts along a found trajectory from its end toward its beginning, cuts standard RL's run time from exponential to **quadratic** in the number of states between rewards ([Salimans & Chen 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.03381)). On a sparse 9-joint pick-and-place task where "PPO… does not encounter a single reward after training in this environment for a billion frames", Go-Explore succeeded by restoring simulator state and exploring at random, then robustified its *own* trajectories with the backward algorithm, using no human demonstrations ([Ecoffet et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12919)). PBCS carries the idea to continuous actions, where "DDPG and TD3 are unable to steer a point mass in even small 2D mazes" ([Matheron et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.11667)), while Latent Go-Explore warns that with an uninformative cell partition "Go-Explore can completely fail" ([Gallouédec & Dellandréa 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.14928)). The honest label for this family is "no demonstrations, privileged resets".

**Self-imitation keeps successes but does not find them.** SIL imitates actions whose return beat the value estimate, and its advantage over PPO grew on delayed-reward MuJoCo ([Oh et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.05635)); transition-level SIL has been criticized for sparse continuous control ([Dai et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.13467)). By inference, SIL needs finishes to exist and a value estimate good enough that "return beat the value" means something. U currently has neither.

## The 2 s planner failed because its value tail never saw its own mistakes

**POLO's bound explains the X2 result almost line by line.** With value error ε, H-step MPC with a terminal value loses at most 2γ^H·ε/(1−γ^H); the authors warn that the arg-max "could inadvertently exploit approximation errors", and their humanoid get-up shows a short horizon finding "a local solution where it can quickly sit up" ([Lowrey et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.01848)). At U's γ, by our arithmetic, that factor is **≈199 at H = 40** and ≈49 at H = 160, and γ^40 ≈ 0.990, so the 2 s planner weighs V(s_H) almost undiscounted and in effect climbs V. Staying on the current tack is U's version of sitting up.

**Every working value-tail planner closes the loop.** POLO refits an ensemble V on states the agent itself visits, with targets from N-step trajectory optimization on the true model and optimism for exploration ([Lowrey et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.01848)). LOOP shows that a mismatch between planner and actor ("actor divergence") destabilizes value learning, and constrains the planner toward the actor ([Sikchi et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.10066)). TD-M(PC)² finds that even closed-loop TD-MPC2 overestimates where the planner rarely goes, by 231% on Dog-Trot and 2,159% on h1hand-run, and that a behaviour-cloning term toward the planner's own actions more than doubles HumanoidBench performance ([Lin et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03550)). X2 had none of these safeguards. Its V was regressed once on 50 demonstrations' returns-to-go and never refit; on held-out RuleSailor episodes its correlation with true returns was 0.27–0.48, and for one seed it was no better than a constant. The planner over-predicted its executed return by **+12.6** on average (correlation 0.12), steered toward states where, in M4X's preliminary analysis, V's per-episode maximum averaged +9.7 to +12.1 (above RuleSailor's best start return of +7.45), and recorded 0/60 tacks and 22 out-of-bounds failures ([M4X report](../docs/ese3990/M4X_report.md)). For the planner track, the inference is a POLO-style V fit on the 2 s planner's own visited states, with the 8 s planner's trajectory optimizations as targets, pessimistic ensemble action selection, and later distillation into a real-time prior ([Wang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18871)). That uses the true model, which is legitimate for the planner but is not the minimal-information RL claim.

**Hierarchy and higher-level action spaces shorten credit chains the most, but inject the most knowledge.** HIRO, HAC and timed-subgoal HiTS rely on a hand-chosen goal space ([Nachum et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.08296); [Gürtler et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.03100)); Director learns latent goals and outperformed exploration methods on sparse tasks ([Hafner et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.04114)); option-critic's options tend to collapse to single steps ([Harb et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.04571)). On action spaces, higher-level parameterizations "can significantly impact the learning, robustness, and quality" of policies ([Peng & van de Panne 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.01055)), and body-rate commands transferred to real quadrotors more robustly than per-rotor thrusts ([Kaufmann et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10796)), but a PPO study found "no single action space is best across all tasks" ([Schneider et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06921)). Our inference is that the defensible framing is an **information ladder**, with each rung reported as injected knowledge: action persistence with no semantics, then absolute rudder and sheet targets instead of rates, then a heading-relative-to-wind setpoint tracked by a disclosed controller (vehicle knowledge), and finally an explicit tack macro like MERL's discrete variant (task knowledge).

## Conclusion

What separates U from the published successes is how finely it cuts time, not how much training or horizon it has. U already matches Bink in simulated training time and roughly matches his horizon in seconds, but it spreads both across ten times as many decisions, under a dense reward worth a thousandth of a point per step, and then asks SAC to learn a soft value whose entropy charge exceeds the whole outcome gap. The project's own controls separate the explanations more sharply than the literature does: 0.5 s holds are enough for the planner; PPO crosses head to wind but never strings together a beat, so the unit to discover is a roughly 50 s beat rather than a single tack; and demonstrations kept permanently in half of every minibatch still decayed, which implicates the critic rather than memory.

Two implications follow. Fix the clock and the critic before adding exploration machinery, because bonuses, SIL and hierarchy all build on a value estimate that is currently thousands of points off. And defend "minimal information" not as a yes-or-no property but as the information ladder: the first rung at which tacking emerges is a publishable result in its own right, because no sailing paper has run that ablation.

## Prioritized experiments: published evidence versus inference

All arms below are post-hoc and exploratory, because U's outcomes are already open, and each deserves an M4X-style freeze before it runs. Common settings: at least **5 seeds**, since one of Bink's four seeds learned sculling instead of tacking; a budget of 200,000 simulated seconds per run (the current 4M decisions); evaluation on the frozen U reward at γ = 0.99975, at 20 Hz, with actions held where an arm uses repetition; and, for every arm, logging finishes out of 20, episodes with a counted tack, mean maximum y, the M4X Q band, and rudder travel to catch sculling.

| Rank | Experiment (concrete change) | Information added | Published evidence | Inference being tested | Pass criterion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Reachability probe, no training.** Roll out random policies on U under white noise at 20 Hz, white noise held for 0.5 s, pink (β = 1), red/OU (β = 2) and ez-greedy zeta holds, sweeping scale; score with `u_diagnostics.py`. | None | Noise type alone flips SAC/TD3 from failure to success on action-cost MountainCar ([Hollenstein 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03787)) | Correlated noise yields tacks and upwind progress without hitting "mines" (out of bounds, capsize). Necessary, not sufficient: random returns there were similar across noise types. | Keep processes with ≥10% of episodes containing a tack and ≤10% failures. |
| 1 | **2×2 factorial: {20 Hz, 0.5 s action repeat} × {SAC, SACLite}, plus PPO at both cadences.** With repeat, γ per block = 0.99975¹⁰ ≈ 0.9975 and the block reward is the discounted sum of its 10 per-decision rewards, so the objective is unchanged. | Persistence only; cadence change disclosed | Frame-skip shrinks the horizon ([Kalyanakrishnan 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.03718)); δt collapse ([Tallec 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.09732)); Bink tacked at 0.5 s; reward inflation and SACLite ([Yu 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.12434)) | Blocks put U at ~400 decisions per horizon (Bink: 500). Above α ≈ 0.011 the entropy charge inverts the outcome ranking; SACLite removes it. | ≥1/20 validation finishes in ≥3/5 seeds; SACLite Q inside [−22.3, +15.7] ± 250. |
| 2 | **Noise color on rank 1's best arm.** SAC: pink noise (`pink-noise-rl`). PPO: β ∈ {0.5, 1, 1.5, 2}. Sweep scale jointly with β. | None | Pink beats white and OU for SAC/MPO ([Eberhard 2023](https://openreview.net/forum?id=hQ9V5QN27eS)); best β of 1.25–2 on MountainCar and maze tasks ([Hollenstein 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11091)) | Rate actions need a lower scale at high β, because noise integrates twice into heading. | More finishing seeds than rank 1 at equal budget. |
| 3 | **Critic objective, if SACLite is not enough:** SACZero; RVI-SAC with a learned reset cost; training γ = 0.999 per decision (0.995 per block) with matched shaping; then a wider ReLU critic with LayerNorm. | None | Average reward and centering help as γ → 1 ([Naik 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.09999); [RVI-SAC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.01972)); SAC at 0.999 worse than at 0.99 on 5/6 tasks; smaller γ regularizes ([Amit 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.02040)) | Removing the γ/(1−γ) offset, not normalization, is what bounds Q; also tests M4X's tanh-versus-ReLU explanation. | Q stays in band throughout; finishes do not fall. |
| 4 | **Reverse-start curriculum.** Grow start states by random 0.5 s holds from states on the gate line; keep starts whose success rate lies in [0.1, 0.9]; evaluate only from the frozen U start. | Privileged reset plus goal location | Reverse curriculum ([Florensa 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.05300)); exponential-to-quadratic speed-up ([Salimans & Chen 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.03381)) | Starting near the gate makes a finish reachable first; pushing starts downwind then *forces* tacks. | Curriculum reaches the true start with ≥5/20 finishes there. |
| 5 | **Go-Explore with state restore, then backward-algorithm robustification into PPO/SAC.** Cells: (x, y) in 2 m bins × heading in 30° bins × a speed bin; explore with random 0.5 s holds. | Privileged restore plus disclosed cell design | Go-Explore solved a task where PPO saw no reward in 1B frames ([Ecoffet 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12919)); PBCS ([Matheron 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.11667)) | H = 160 MPC shows finishing paths exist within 8 s windows, so the archive should find a gate crossing quickly. | Archive finds a gate crossing; robustified policy reaches ≥5/20 finishes. |
| 6 | **Success buffer or SIL** (separate buffer of finished episodes with Monte Carlo returns), added to the first arm that finishes at all. | None | SIL's advantage grows with reward delay ([Oh 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.05635)); dedicated rewarded-transition buffer in ETGL-DDPG ([Futuhi 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05225)) | Retention matters only once discovery and the critic work; the demonstration-arm decay says it is not the first bottleneck. | Final checkpoint keeps ≥80% of peak finish rate. |
| 7 | **Annealed count bonus or MaxInfoRL**, only if ranks 1–5 produce no finishes. | None | MaxInfoRL repairs SAC under action costs ([Sukhija 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12098)); small gains at fixed budget ([Taïga 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02388)) | Novelty may reward reaching across rather than tacking. | Any finish where ranks 1–5 had none. |
| 8 | **Closed-loop value tail for the 2 s planner.** Fit an ensemble V on states the H = 40 planner visits, with H = 160 trajectory-optimization targets, refit every K episodes; act on mean − β·std; add an actor/prior regularizer. | True model (planner track) | POLO's bound and closed loop ([Lowrey 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.01848)); actor divergence ([LOOP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.10066)); planner overestimation ([TD-M(PC)²](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03550)) | X2's +12.6 optimism is an open-loop artefact; a refit V that ranks mid-tack states above fast states on the current tack lets a 2 s planner tack. | ≥1/60 tacks, then finishes near H = 160 at <50 ms p50; mean prediction gap under 2. |
| 9 | **Information ladder:** absolute rudder and sheet targets → heading-to-wind setpoint → explicit tack macro. | Increasing, disclosed | Action parameterization matters ([Peng 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.01055); [Kaufmann 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10796)) but no space is best everywhere ([Schneider 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06921)); MERL's tack macro | Locates the lowest rung at which tacking emerges unaided. | Report finishes per rung. |
| 10 | **Reward-level change, as a task change:** Bink-style timeout penalty with remaining time added to the observation, or a level-based upwind term; evaluate on the frozen reward. | Changes the task | Timeout penalty and level reward in Bink; time-awareness required when timeouts are terminal ([Pardo 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.00378)); shaping can mislead ([HER](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.01495)) | A dense signal of ~10⁻³ per decision is too weak to guide search. | More finishes under the *frozen* evaluation. |
