# Physics invariants — the brief §35 suite, with tolerances

Every item brief §35 requires, the test that proves it, the tolerance that test
asserts, and **why that tolerance is the right number**. A tolerance chosen to
make a failing test pass is a defect; this column is where that becomes
visible, so each one states either that the comparison is exact or what the
worst residual actually measured is.

Two tests keep the table honest and are themselves rows in it:

- `crates/sailgym-physics/tests/invariants.rs::documented_invariants_exist`
  reads this file, checks that every test it names exists in the file it names,
  and that every `#[test]` in the invariant suite has a row here. The document
  cannot drift from the suite in either direction.
- `crates/sailgym-physics/tests/provenance.rs::docs_match_source` does the same
  job for `docs/parameters.md` (task 10.7).

Gate step 4 runs `--test invariants --test no_shortcuts --test convergence
--test symmetry`. All figures below were measured on the section 10 host
(`rustc 1.98.1`, `x86_64-unknown-linux-gnu`, debug profile); the margins are
the same in release.

## brief §35, item by item

| Invariant (brief §35) | Test | Tolerance | Why that tolerance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rest equilibrium | `crates/sailgym-physics/tests/invariants.rs::rest_equilibrium` | **exact** (0 ULP on 12 of 13 fields, 10 000 steps) | With no wind and neutral controls every force term is *structurally* zero, not merely small: `foil_force` returns `Vec2::ZERO` below `EPS_FLOW` (F5.3), the F6.6 hull terms are products with zero velocities, and `GZ(0) = 0` exactly because every term of the series carries one factor of `sin φ` (F6.7). The derivative is therefore exactly `0.0` and `axpy` adds `dt·0.0`. There is nothing to round, so any non-zero residual is a real drift and a tolerance would hide it. `t` is excluded because `ṫ = 1` by F3. |
| Port/starboard mirror symmetry — hull and foils | `crates/sailgym-physics/tests/invariants.rs::mirror_symmetry_trajectory` | 1e-10 abs, every field, every step of 20 s (4 000 steps) | Measured worst residual: **exactly 0.0**. `cl` is bit-exactly odd and `cd` bit-exactly even (`foil.rs` branches on no sign, section 04), and IEEE negation, multiplication and addition are all sign-symmetric, so the mirrored arithmetic is the exact negation term for term. The bound is not zero only because a future change to the frame code could legitimately introduce one rounding step; 1e-10 m over 20 s of a boat travelling tens of metres is still ten decades below anything physical. |
| Port/starboard mirror symmetry — with the sail | `crates/sailgym-physics/tests/invariants.rs::sail_mirror_symmetry_trajectory` | 1e-9 abs, every field, every step of 30 s (6 000 steps) | Measured worst residual **1.60e-14**, a margin of ≈ 6×10⁴. Not exact, because the wind bearing enters through `wind_from_bearing`, where `sin`/`cos` of 135° and of 45° are not exact negations of each other; that seeds a ~1 ULP asymmetry which the 6 000-step trajectory amplifies to 1e-14. 1e-9 leaves room for that growth to be four decades worse before the test fires, and is still far below a millimetre. |
| Port/starboard mirror symmetry — through a capsize | `crates/sailgym-physics/tests/invariants.rs::roll_mirror_symmetry` | 1e-9 abs on `φ` and `p`, every step of 30 s | Measured worst residual **1.41e-15**, a margin of ≈ 7×10⁵. Same mechanism and same reasoning as the row above; the run is asserted to exceed 1 rad of heel, so the nonlinear part of `GZ` is what is being mirrored, not just its linear root. |
| Sheet unilateral constraint — along a trajectory | `crates/sailgym-physics/tests/invariants.rs::sheet_unilateral_constraint` | **exact** `T ≥ 0`, 50 × 60 s episodes = 600 000 steps | `T = max(0, k·e + c·ė)` (F6.8). `max` with `0.0` cannot return a negative number, so the bound is the constant, not an approximation to one. |
| Sheet unilateral constraint — as a property of the element | `crates/sailgym-physics/tests/invariants.rs::tension_never_negative` | **exact** `T ≥ 0`; slack ⇒ `T`, `M_β` and both loads exactly `0.0`; 200 000 randomised cases, one in seven from impossible ranges | Same structural argument. The exact-zero half is what proves there is no slackness branch: a rope with `e < 0` produces no force through the *same* expression that carries a loaded one. Both sides of the `max` are asserted to have been taken, so the test is not a statement about one branch. |
| Force sign sanity | `crates/sailgym-physics/tests/invariants.rs::force_sign_sanity` | **exact** sign test, `g·velocity ≤ 0` on all four DOF, 200 randomised states | A sign is not a magnitude; there is no tolerance to choose. The foils are switched off by zeroing their areas and the righting arm by zeroing `GM`/`GZ_max`, because a foil's lift is perpendicular to its own local flow and `K_restore` is a function of `φ` rather than of `p`: neither has a sign relation to the body velocity, so including them would test a proposition that is not true. Both have their own sign guards (`hydrostatics::restoring_moment_sign`, `roll_mirror_symmetry`). |
| Velocity scaling `F ∝ V²` | `crates/sailgym-physics/tests/invariants.rs::velocity_scaling` | 2 % relative, 3 rudder angles × 3 speed ratios × 2 foils | Measured worst deviation **1.97e-16 relative** — i.e. round-off. At a fixed angle of attack `foil_force` is exactly `q·A·C(α)` with `q = ½ρV²`, so the ratio is exact; the 2 % the PRD asks for is the bound appropriate to the *physical* claim ("approximately", brief §35) rather than to this implementation, and is left as written so that a future foil model with a Reynolds-number term still has to be close. The hull is deliberately excluded: F6.6 is linear + quadratic by construction and is not claimed to scale as `V²`. |
| Zero-flow foil behaviour | `crates/sailgym-physics/tests/invariants.rs::zero_flow_foil_behaviour` | **exact** `Vec3::ZERO`, three rudder angles | F5.3 short-circuits below `EPS_FLOW = 1e-9 m/s` and returns the zero vector, so "approaches zero" is implemented as "is zero". `assert_eq!` on the vector is the strongest available statement and costs nothing. |
| Dissipative behaviour — no wind, no roll | `crates/sailgym-physics/tests/invariants.rs::dissipative_behaviour` | +1e-9 J per step, 50 × 5 s episodes | Measured worst per-step change **−2.48e-4 J**: energy never rose at all, in any step of any episode. The bound is one-sided and is the PRD's; the margin is that the largest *rise* is negative. The account is complete — kinetic, boom, sheet elastic and the F6.7 roll potential — because an incomplete one reports energy vanishing into a conservative store and reappearing out of it. |
| Dissipative behaviour — with roll live | `crates/sailgym-physics/tests/invariants.rs::dissipative_with_roll` | +1e-9 J per step, 40 × 20 s episodes with randomised heel | Measured worst per-step change **−2.55e-5 J**; again never positive. Hydrostatic righting is conservative, so it moves energy between the kinetic and roll-potential stores; the test asserts the total never grows and that each episode ends below where it started. |
| Dissipative behaviour — the sheet specifically | `crates/sailgym-physics/tests/invariants.rs::sheet_does_no_negative_work` | +1e-9 J per step, 10 × 20 s episodes | Measured worst per-step change **−3.73e-9 J**; never positive. **This is the one row whose fixture is restricted, and it is restricted on the record.** Sheet lengths are drawn above `ℓ(0) = 1.0404 m`. Below that — which the shipped `l_sheet_min = 0.90 m` is — the rope is permanently stretched at `β = 0`, where `dℓ/dβ = 0` and the element's own damping vanishes, leaving an undamped ≈ 43 rad/s mode that explicit RK2 pumps at `+2.4e-3 J` per step at `dt = 0.005`. That rise falls off as `dt³` (0.138 J at `dt = 0.01`, 3.1e-6 J at `dt = 0.00125`), which identifies it as integrator truncation rather than an accounting error. The bound was **not** widened to accommodate it; the fixture states its premise instead, and `docs/progress/06-handoff.md` §2.5 and §4 carry the measurements and the recommended parameter fix. |
| Coordinate-frame consistency | `crates/sailgym-physics/tests/invariants.rs::coordinate_frame_consistency` | world position 1e-9 m, body-frame `u, v, r, p` 1e-11, 3 rotations × 4 000 steps | Measured worst: **7.82e-14 m** in position (margin ≈ 1×10⁴) and **3.22e-14** in the body quantities (margin ≈ 300). The residual is real and is not round-off in the comparison: rotating the setup rotates the wind bearing too, and `sin`/`cos` at 30°, 90° and 217° are not related to those at 0° by any exact identity. The body tolerance is two decades tighter than the world one because body quantities must be **unchanged**, not rotated — that is the actual content of the invariant, and a looser bound there would let a genuine frame error through. |
| Coordinate-frame consistency — systematically | `crates/sailgym-physics/tests/symmetry.rs::rotation_and_mirror_sweep` | body 1e-11, world position 1e-9, mirror 1e-9; 6 scenarios × 8 rotations × 2 mirror states = 96 cases | Task 10.3. The bounds are the same numbers as the row above and were chosen the same way; the sweep is what makes them a statement about the whole shipped scenario set rather than one hand-picked fixture. Worst residuals across all 96 cases are recorded in `docs/progress/10-handoff.md`. |
| Time-step convergence | `crates/sailgym-physics/tests/convergence.rs::timestep_convergence` | observed order of accuracy in `[1.7, 2.3]` | Task 10.2. RK2 midpoint is second order, so the interval brackets 2.0 with ±0.3 — wide enough that the finite-difference order estimate's own error (the next term in the expansion is `O(h³)`, so the estimate carries an `O(h)` bias) does not fire it, narrow enough to exclude first order (1.0) and third (3.0), which is the only thing a convergence test can usefully distinguish. |
| Time-step convergence — absolute error at the default | `crates/sailgym-physics/tests/convergence.rs::error_at_default_dt` | position < 0.05 m, heel < 0.5° over 20 s against an `Rk4` reference at `dt = 3.125e-4` | Task 10.2. The PRD's figures, and they are physical rather than numerical: 5 cm over a 20 s run of a 4.23 m boat, and half a degree of heel against a `phi_capsize` of 80°. Measured values and the reference's own convergence are in `docs/convergence.md`. |
| Deterministic replay | `crates/sailgym-physics/tests/invariants.rs::deterministic_replay` | **exact** (0 ULP) over all 38 scalars of ~600 frames, two scenarios | brief §34 is a bit-identity requirement, so the only correct tolerance is none. Compared with `to_bits()`, through the section 09 recorder rather than only the state, and the episode is asserted to have loaded the rig and seen the release command — a replay of a boat that did nothing would be trivially identical. Cross-platform identity is **not** claimed (F9). |
| Deterministic replay — the shipped trajectories | `crates/sailgym-physics/tests/regression.rs::beam_reach_capsize` | 1e-9 on positions and angles, 1e-10 on `u, v, r, p, β̇`, 151 samples | Section 09's goldens. Measured worst |Δ| on the recording build: **0** at every sample of all six scenarios. The tolerances are non-zero only because a golden file stores decimal text; `float_roundtrip` makes that exact today, so the margin is the whole tolerance. A 0.1 % change to one hull coefficient fails all six within 0.2 s of simulated time (`docs/progress/09-handoff.md` §4). |
| Finite-number invariant | `crates/sailgym-physics/tests/invariants.rs::finite_number_invariant` | **exact** `is_finite()`, 200 × 30 s episodes under randomised loads and controls | `NaN`/`Inf` is a predicate, not a quantity. |
| Finite-number invariant — past capsize | `crates/sailgym-physics/tests/invariants.rs::capsize_finite` | **exact** `is_finite()`, 100 × 60 s episodes at 8–22 m/s; > 50 must actually pass `phi_capsize` | brief §17's regime, where a clamp or a termination would be the easy wrong answer. The episode count that must go over is asserted so the test cannot pass by never reaching the case it is about. |

## Invariants this project added beyond brief §35

These are not in the brief's list. They guard conventions and prohibitions that
the brief states in prose elsewhere, and they are part of the same suite.

| Invariant | Source | Test | Tolerance | Why that tolerance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The sail angle is never commanded | brief §9, §11 | `crates/sailgym-physics/tests/invariants.rs::no_sail_angle_command` | **exact** — a source grep; zero assignments to `.beta` outside `integrator.rs`/`state.rs` | A prohibition, not a measurement. The needle is an assignment operator after `.beta` in whitespace-stripped source, so `==` and `+=` are told apart. |
| Apparent wind matches an independent derivation | brief §8, F6.2 | `crates/sailgym-physics/tests/invariants.rs::apparent_wind_consistency` | 1e-12 on the vector, the speed and the angle, 200 steps | Measured worst residual **exactly 0.0**: the independent CG derivation performs the same multiplications in the same order, so it agrees to the bit. 1e-12 allows a future reordering inside `apparent_wind_at` without failing, while staying six decades below anything a knot of wind could mean. |
| The boom crosses continuously through a tack | brief §9, §46 | `crates/sailgym-physics/tests/invariants.rs::tack_through_wind` | no step in `β` larger than 0.3 rad between consecutive physics steps, 40 s | Measured worst step **0.0177 rad**, a margin of ≈ 17×. 0.3 rad is the PRD's figure and is set by what it is guarding against: a hard-coded tack state would move the boom across in one step (≈ 2 rad), so any bound well under that catches it, and one well above the physical peak rate (`|β̇| ≈ 3.5 rad/s × dt = 0.018 rad`) does not fire on legitimate motion. |
| A slack sheet leaves the boom free | brief §11 | `crates/sailgym-physics/tests/invariants.rs::slack_sheet_free_boom` | 1e-9 on `β` and `β̇` against `I_b β̈ = −c_β β̇` under the same RK2 map, 30 s; tension exactly `0.0` | Measured worst residual **3.23e-14**, a margin of ≈ 3×10⁴. The reference is the closed-form RK2 map of the pure damping ODE, so what is compared is the physics and not the integrator; the residual is the difference between evaluating that map directly and reaching it through `evaluate`. |
| The sheet's boom torque is continuous | brief §11 | `crates/sailgym-physics/tests/invariants.rs::sheet_geometry_continuous` | no step larger than 1 N·m over 1e-5 rad at `l_sheet_max`; **and** halving the sample spacing must more than halve the largest step, at five sheet lengths | Measured worst step **0.623 N·m** at `l_sheet_max`. The 1 N·m figure is a bound on `|dM/dβ|` of 1e5 N·m/rad, which the F7 rope stiffness exceeds at most *other* lengths — so the second check exists: a slope shrinks with the spacing and a discontinuity does not. That is a proof of continuity independent of how stiff the element is, and it covers the hauled lengths the first check cannot. |
| Heel reduces drive, geometrically | brief §10, F6.4 | `crates/sailgym-physics/tests/invariants.rs::heel_reduces_drive` | **exact** inequality on `total.x` and on the sail's roll contribution | An ordering, not a magnitude. The point is that the reduction comes out of `R_x(−φ)` and `Generalized::add` rather than an empirical `cos φ`; a numeric target would invite one to be fitted. |
| The document and the suite agree | F13.4 | `crates/sailgym-physics/tests/invariants.rs::documented_invariants_exist` | **exact** — set equality, both directions | An audit. |

## What is *not* asserted here, and where it lives instead

- **Cross-platform bit-identity.** Not claimed (F9). The golden regressions
  skip, with a message naming both toolchains, on any build but the one that
  recorded them.
- **Quantitative agreement with a real ILCA.** Explicitly out of scope
  (brief §36); see `docs/parameters.md`.
- **Prohibited shortcuts** (no manoeuvre state, no capsize branch, no heel
  clamp, no release-recovery rule, no linear righting spring) are five source
  audits in `crates/sailgym-physics/tests/no_shortcuts.rs`, run by the same
  gate step. They are prohibitions rather than invariants, so they have their
  own target.
- **The F7 parameter boundary** (no physical literal outside `parameters.rs`
  and `constants.rs`, no physics in TypeScript) is
  `crates/sailgym-physics/tests/provenance.rs`.
