# Multi-core throughput

Generated by `cargo run --release -p sailgym-bench --bin vec_bench -- --write docs/v2/throughput.md`. **Do not edit by hand.** Every figure is produced by that command, in the house style of [`../v1/performance.md`](../v1/performance.md).

`discussions/cross-stack.md` §0 says to measure before porting anything. `../v1/performance.md` records **3 144–3 532× real time on one core** for a single environment. This document answers the other half: what more than one core buys, across N independent boats.

**What is parallel here, and what is not.** F9.6 forbids parallelism *inside* a single simulation step and is unchanged. These are N **independent** `Simulation`s stepped on N threads: they share no accumulator, no field and no RNG, so each performs exactly the arithmetic it would perform alone. v2 F16.5 permits that, in `sailgym-env` and `sailgym-bench` only — **`rayon` may not appear in `sailgym-physics`**. The binary does not take that on trust: for every N and every thread count it compares the parallel run's final states with the serial run's using `to_bits()`, and a single differing bit fails the run before a figure is printed. Every point below passed.

## The answer

At the largest population, **N = 4096**, the best point is **24 threads** at **5.251 M steps/s**, which is **26253× real time** in aggregate. The one-thread rayon column reads 0.578 M steps/s, so the speed-up is **9.1×** on 24 threads (38 % efficiency), and the serial baseline in the same process reads 0.575 M steps/s — a harness cost of -0.5 % against the one-thread pool.

One environment at 3017× real time (`../v1/performance.md`) becomes 26253× across 4096 of them on this machine. Read that against what an RL run actually needs before treating a port as a throughput decision: `cross-stack.md` §0's question is answered with a number here, and the section handoff states the verdict in one sentence.

---

## What this does not measure

- **Bare `Simulation`s, not episodes.** There is no `sailgym-env` yet, and the answer was wanted before that crate was designed rather than after. Nothing here includes the cost of building an observation, of an agent's decision cadence, or of crossing into Python. Section 06 re-measures on the real runner.
- **One scenario.** `gybe` is the slowest of the six in `../v1/performance.md` because it is the only gusty one; a uniform-wind population is roughly 11 % faster per step.
- **One machine.** The host block above is the whole of the claim's scope.
- **No interaction between boats.** F16.5: the moment boats interact through a shared field, the parallel path returns to a fixed index order or to an explicit two-phase sample-then-step, and these figures stop applying.

### The answer, on the real runner

At the largest population, **N = 4096**, the best point is **24 threads** at **4.094 M steps/s** and **409.4 k agent-decisions/s**, which is **20472× real time** in aggregate.

The bare-`Simulation` baseline measured in the same process at the same N reads **0.571 M steps/s** on one thread, so `VecEnv` runs at **0.70×** the bare rate per step (0.398 against 0.571 M steps/s serial) — a step costs **1.44×** what a bare one costs. Across all threads the runner reaches **7.17×** the single-threaded bare figure.

**Where that cost is**, from the two cadences rather than from an argument. Solving the two serial rates for a per-step cost and a per-decision cost gives **2319 ns per physics step** and **1947 ns per decision**, against **1750 ns** for a bare step. At cadence 10 the decision contributes 195 ns per step, which is **25 %** of the difference: the rest is the per-step loop itself. `Outcome` is evaluated after **every** physics step, so the episode calls `Simulation::advance(1)` rather than `advance(period)` — and `advance` refreshes its cached force breakdown once per call, so a per-step loop pays one extra force evaluation per step on top of RK2's two. That is what buys a capsize resolved on the step it happens and a mark passage tested at the physics rate rather than at the sample rate.

### What this section does not measure

- **No controller.** The action source is `manual` with one held action per slot. A rule sailor or a policy adds its own cost and none exists yet.
- **No recording and no decision log.** Both were switched off; the decision log allocates per decision and the recorder builds a `Diagnostics`.
- **No route.** `Outcome` is evaluated every step either way, but the mark-passage probe and the guidance sensor's course arithmetic are not in these figures.
- **No Python.** Section 07 measures the binding, separately (F17.5).
- **One machine, one scenario.** As above.
