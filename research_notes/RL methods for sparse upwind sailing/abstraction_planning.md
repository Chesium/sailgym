## Q1. MPC/planning with learned terminal values: how V is learned, and how methods stop the planner exploiting V's errors

### Takeaway
Every successful value-tail planner (POLO, LOOP, TD-MPC/TD-MPC2, MuZero) **closes the loop**. V is re-fitted continually, by bootstrapping on the states and actions the planner itself visits, so any state whose value V overestimates gets visited, receives a lower target and is corrected. Most of these methods also add an uncertainty or anti-exploitation device: an ensemble with optimism for exploration and pessimism for evaluation, min-of-Q, or a constraint that keeps the planner near the policy or data distribution. A fixed V regressed on demonstration returns-to-go has none of these properties. The literature names the failure we saw: "the arg max … could inadvertently exploit approximation errors" (POLO); the related distribution-shift failures are "actor divergence" (LOOP) and "policy mismatch" (TD-M(PC)²).

### Cited Findings

**POLO: Plan Online, Learn Offline (Lowrey, Rajeswaran, Kakade, Todorov, Mordatch; ICLR 2019)**
- Lemma 1 (greedy policy, after Singh & Yee): with value error ε = max_s |V̂(s) − V*(s)|, J(π*) − J(π̂) ≤ 2γε/(1−γ). The bound is tight for some MDPs. The authors warn that "the arg max operation in π̂ could inadvertently exploit approximation errors to produce a poor policy" and that fitted-VI depends critically on the sampling distribution, "especially in sparse reward settings" — [POLO, arXiv 1811.01848](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.01848)
- Lemma 2 (H-step MPC with terminal reward r_f = V̂): J(π*) − J(π̂_MPC) ≤ 2γ^H ε/(1−γ^H). So MPC is "less susceptible to approximation errors than greedy action selection". Without a terminal value, ε = O(r_max/(1−γ)) in the worst case — [POLO](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.01848)
- **How V is learned:** from the agent's own experience only. Every visited state goes into a replay buffer. Every Z = 16 steps, K value networks are re-fitted with targets y_k(s) = max_{a_0:N−1} E[Σ_{t<N} γ^t r_t + γ^N V̂_θk(s_N)]. Each target is an **N-step trajectory optimization** on the known model, so the targets are planner-bootstrapped and not Monte-Carlo returns of a fixed behavior policy. A larger N "learns the value function with fewer interactions" and makes the targets more stable — [POLO](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.01848)
- **Uncertainty and optimism:** K networks are trained with randomized priors (Osband-style posterior sampling). The planner uses V̂(s) = log Σ_k exp(κ V̂_θk(s)), which "approximates mean + variance for small κ". This makes the planner seek states where the ensemble disagrees ("optimism in the face of uncertainty"). The ensemble "recognize[s] the true, low value of visited states, while preserving an optimistic value elsewhere" — [POLO](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.01848)
- **Assumptions and compute:** POLO assumes the agent has an **internal (true) model**; MuJoCo is the model. The humanoid used MPPI with H = 64, 120 rollouts, noise σ = 0.2, temperature 1.25 and γ = 0.99. The value function was an ensemble of 6 tiny MLPs (2×16, tanh). Total agent lifetime was 12,000 steps (96 s of experience). The in-hand manipulation task used 80 MPPI trajectories — [POLO](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.01848)
- **Evidence most relevant to tacking:** "With a short planning horizon, the humanoid getup task has a local solution where it can quickly sit up, but cannot discover a chain of actions required to stand upright. POLO's exploration allows the agent to escape the local solution." POLO dominated pure MPC of the same horizon in the getup and in-hand tasks. In a 2-D point-mass exploration task, 32-step POLO covered more area than 1-step (greedy) POLO and than random exploration — [POLO](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.01848)
- POLO's learned V was also used with **no per-step reward** (MPC with H = 64 maximizing only V(s_64)), and the humanoid still rose. But "a greedy optimization procedure with this value function does not yield good results, indicating that the learned value function is only approximate and not good everywhere". A longer horizon mitigated V degradation under model perturbation (Fig. 4b) — [POLO](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.01848)

**LOOP: Learning Off-Policy with Online Planning (Sikchi, Zhou, Held; CoRL 2021)**
- Theorem 1: J(π*) − J(π_H,V̂) ≤ 2/(1−γ^H) · [C(ε_m, H, γ) + ε_p/2 + γ^H ε_v], where C grows with the model's TV error ε_m and with H. The H-step lookahead cuts the dependence on value error by at least γ^{H−1} but adds a model-error term. The lookahead "is beneficial when the value-function bias dominates the bias in the learned model" — [LOOP, arXiv 2008.10066](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.10066)
- **How V is learned:** with an off-policy model-free learner (SAC; a SARSA variant was also tested) on the replay buffer of the planner's own data. The model is a learned dynamics ensemble — [LOOP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.10066)
- **Actor divergence:** data come from the H-step lookahead policy, but the Bellman backup uses the parametric actor π_θ. The resulting distribution shift "can lead to accumulated bootstrapping errors … and destabilize value learning". The fix is **Actor Regularized Control (ARC)**, which constrains trajectory optimization to stay close to a prior β·π_θ + (1−β)·N(μ_{t−1}, σ). Without ARC, training "becomes unstable" (Walker ablation) — [LOOP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.10066)
- Offline-LOOP uses a **pessimistic** objective, mean_K[R_H,V̂] − β_pess·std_K[R_H,V̂], over the model ensemble. More optimization iterations with a loose prior "may potentially select trajectories that lead to out-of-distribution states with overestimated value" — [LOOP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.10066)
- Settings: H = 3 (8 for the safety environments). PETS-restricted (CEM with the same H = 3, no value) "performs poorly due to myopic reasoning over a limited horizon". ARC runs at **14.3 Hz** on HalfCheetah (Xeon E5-2620 v4 + GTX 1080 Ti). LOOP-SAC was much more sample-efficient than SAC and competitive with MBPO on locomotion — [LOOP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.10066)

**Blending MPC & Value Function Approximation (Bhardwaj, Choudhury, Boots; ICLR 2021)**
- The paper frames MPC as a sequence of local Q-function approximations and blends MPC and the learned value with a TD(λ)-like λ. λ is adapted over time, moving reliance from MPC toward the learned value as it improves, which balances model error against value error. On manipulation tasks with biased models it was "comparable with MPC with access to true dynamics even under severe model bias" and more sample-efficient than model-free RL — [Bhardwaj et al., arXiv 2012.05909](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.05909)
- Precursor: MPQ (Information-Theoretic Model Predictive Q-Learning; Bhardwaj, Handa, Fox, Boots; L4DC 2020) learns a soft-Q terminal value for MPPI from the agent's own interaction — [arXiv 2001.02153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.02153) (abstract-level, not re-fetched)

**TD-MPC (ICML 2022) and TD-MPC2 (Hansen, Su, Wang; ICLR 2024)**
- TD-MPC2 plans with **H = 3** latent steps. MPPI uses 512 samples, 64 elites, 6 iterations (+2 when the action has ≥20 dims), 24 samples from the policy prior, and a warm start. A learned Q bootstraps returns beyond the horizon — [TD-MPC2, arXiv 2310.16828](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16828)
- **How Q is learned:** by TD on replay data collected by the planner. Targets are r + γ·Q_target(z′, π(z′)), with the next action taken from the **policy prior** and not the planner. Five Q-functions with EMA targets; each target is the **min of 2 randomly sub-sampled Qs** (pessimism against overestimation); discrete regression over 101 log-transformed bins. Decoder-free latent model with SimNorm — [TD-MPC2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16828)
- Heuristic discount: γ = clip((T/5 − 1)/(T/5), [0.95, 0.995]), where T is episode length after action repeat. DMControl uses action repeat 2 (T = 500), giving γ = 0.99 — [TD-MPC2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16828)
- Results: 104 tasks with one hyperparameter set; beats SAC, DreamerV3 and TD-MPC. A 317M multitask model took ≈33 GPU-days on one RTX 3090. The paper does not discuss exploration limits — [TD-MPC2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16828)
- TD-MPC (v1): latent model, terminal Q, short horizon (H = 5) — [arXiv 2203.04955](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.04955) (abstract-level)

**2025: value overestimation in planner-plus-value systems (TD-M(PC)²; Lin, Wang, Schneider, Shi; arXiv Feb 2025)**
- TD-MPC2's value **persistently overestimates** because of "policy mismatch": the planner generates the data, but targets bootstrap from the policy prior, so "errors in regions the planner rarely visits go uncorrected and compound". Measured value error relative to true return: Hopper-Stand 15%, Dog-Trot 231%, h1hand-run 2159%, h1hand-slide 746% — [TD-M(PC)², arXiv 2502.03550](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03550)
- The fix is a TD3+BC-style log-likelihood term pulling the policy toward replay-buffer (planner) actions. It adds no compute. More than doubles mean final HumanoidBench performance versus TD-MPC2, with small gains on DMControl. A pure-BC variant is "nearly on par", which suggests that **removing out-of-distribution action queries** drives most of the gain. Uses H = 3, MPPI with 512 samples and 6 iterations — [TD-M(PC)²](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03550)
- Bootstrapped MPC (BMPC; Wang et al., ICLR 2025) addresses the same mismatch by training the policy to imitate the MPC planner and the value on that imitated behavior — [arXiv 2503.18871](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.18871) (from prior knowledge, not re-fetched)

**MuZero-style planning for continuous control**
- MuZero plans with MCTS in a learned latent model whose value/policy/reward heads are trained on its own search data (value targets are n-step bootstrapped returns) — [Schrittwieser et al., Nature 2020, arXiv 1911.08265](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.08265) (abstract-level)
- Sampled MuZero extends this to large and continuous action spaces by sampling actions for policy improvement inside the search; it was evaluated on DeepMind Control Suite and the Real-World RL suite — [Hubert et al., ICML 2021, arXiv 2104.06303](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06303) (abstract-level)
- Hamrick et al. ("On the role of planning in model-based deep RL", ICLR 2021) found that planning helps most **during learning** (better policy-update targets and a better data distribution), that shallow search is often enough at test time, and that planning alone does not generalize — [arXiv 2011.04021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.04021) (abstract-level)

### Inferences
- **Why our value-tail planner failed (mechanism):**
  1. *Wrong target.* Regressing on 50 demonstrations' returns-to-go estimates V^demo (the demonstrator's value) and only on demo-visited states. POLO, LOOP and TD-MPC fit a bootstrapped V on the states the planner itself visits; we fit V on states the planner never visits once it deviates.
  2. *No feedback loop.* V is fixed, so an overestimated state is never corrected. POLO's ensemble "recognizes the true, low value of visited states". TD-M(PC)² shows that even a closed-loop system overestimates where the planner rarely goes; open loop is strictly worse. Our measured +12.6 gap between predicted and executed return is the same symptom.
  3. *Small γ^H attenuation at our discount.* With γ = 0.99975, Lemma 2's amplification factor 2γ^H/(1−γ^H) is ≈7,998 at H = 1, ≈399 at H = 20 (2 s), ≈199 at H = 40 and ≈99 at H = 80 (8 s) (computed from POLO's formula). γ^20 ≈ 0.995, so the 2 s planner weights V(s_H) almost undiscounted. The small dense shaping over 20 steps is negligible next to V, and the planner effectively runs a **pure V-ascent over 2 s**. POLO itself notes that the greedy (H = 1) version of such a V "does not yield good results".
  4. *Credit ranking across a tack.* To start a tack inside a 2 s window, V must rank "mid-tack, momentarily slow, on the new board" above "fast on the current board". Demonstration RTG data rarely contain matched counterfactual pairs, so the OOD region the planner reaches is exactly where V is least constrained. The planner picks continuations that land where V is spuriously high and not ones that tack.
- **What the literature would change, in rough order of cost:**
  - (a) Make V bootstrapped from the agent's own planner data, POLO style. We have the **true model** (POLO's assumption), so the 8 s CEM planner, which already tacks by itself, can generate N-step trajectory-optimization targets offline, and V can be refitted iteratively on the states the short-horizon planner actually visits. This is legitimately "the agent's own experience": no demonstrations.
  - (b) Use an ensemble V with **pessimism (mean − β·std or min-of-K) for action selection** and optimism (log-sum-exp) only for exploratory episodes.
  - (c) Constrain the short planner to a prior or the data support (ARC, a TD-M(PC)² style BC term), so it cannot drive s_H off-distribution.
  - (d) Distill the slow 8 s planner into a policy prior plus V (BMPC / expert-iteration style) and run a cheap 2 s planner warm-started from that prior at 50 ms.
- Our 8 s versus 2 s result matches POLO's humanoid finding (short horizon gets stuck in a local optimum: "sit up" there, "stay on current tack" here) and LOOP's PETS-restricted finding (myopic short-horizon planning fails without a good tail).
- Compute reference: published value-tail planners use very short model horizons (H = 3 to 5 for LOOP and TD-MPC/TD-MPC2; H = 64 for POLO with the true model) and run at roughly 14 Hz on 2017–2020 GPUs (LOOP). With a good V, a 2 s or shorter horizon with blocked actions is in line with the field.

### Gaps
- POLO's in-hand manipulation numbers and exact humanoid curves are only in figures; no tabulated numbers were extracted.
- I found no paper that runs a value-tail planner with γ as close to 1 as 0.99975 at 10 Hz. TD-MPC2's heuristic caps γ at 0.995, and DreamerV3 uses 0.997, so our setting is outside the tested ranges.
- BMPC, MPQ, MuZero, Sampled MuZero and Hamrick et al. were not re-fetched; their claims are at abstract level from prior knowledge.
- I did not find quantitative "predicted vs executed return" exploitation measurements in the planning literature other than TD-M(PC)²'s value-error percentages.

## Q2. Model-based RL for long horizons: Dreamer/DreamerV3, MBPO, PETS; horizon limits and action repeat

### Takeaway
All mainstream MBRL methods keep **model rollouts short**: Dreamer 15–16 imagined steps, MBPO "short branched rollouts", PETS/LOOP-style CEM a few tens of steps or fewer, TD-MPC2 3 steps. They reach long effective horizons through a **bootstrapped critic** (λ-returns), a discount of ≈0.99–0.997 and **action repeat**. None relies on a fixed, externally fitted value. DreamerV3's Minecraft diamond result is the strongest evidence that a world model plus a bootstrapped critic can discover long multi-step behavior from sparse reward without demonstrations, at about 100M steps on one GPU.

### Cited Findings
- **DreamerV3** (Hafner, Pasukonis, Ba, Lillicrap; arXiv 2023, published in Nature 2025). Imagination horizon T = 16; γ = 0.997; the critic learns the distribution of bootstrapped λ-returns. Returns are normalized by an EMA (0.99) of the 5th–95th percentile range of λ-returns, dividing by max(1, S) so that small (sparse) returns are not amplified. Vector observations use symlog; reward and critic heads use a symexp two-hot loss over exponentially spaced bins. "All the Dreamer agents we trained on Minecraft discover diamonds in 100M environment steps" without human data or curricula. One A100 per agent; the Minecraft result is cited at 1 GPU × 9 days — [DreamerV3, arXiv 2301.04104](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04104)
- The DreamerV3 paper notes that alternative return normalizations "struggle under sparse rewards"; the percentile normalization exists for that reason — [DreamerV3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04104)
- **Dreamer (v1)** (Hafner et al., ICLR 2020) learns behaviors by back-propagating analytic value gradients through imagined latent trajectories, with a learned value covering returns beyond the imagination horizon — [arXiv 1912.01603](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.01603) (abstract-level; the action repeat of 2 for DMC and imagination horizon of 15 are prior knowledge, not re-verified)
- TD-MPC2 uses **action repeat 2** for DMControl and sets γ from the post-repeat episode length — [TD-MPC2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16828)
- **MBPO** (Janner, Fu, Zhang, Levine; NeurIPS 2019) argues that "short model-generated rollouts branched from real data" keep the benefits of model-based RL while avoiding compounding model error. A model-free learner (SAC) learns the long-horizon value from these short rollouts — [arXiv 1906.08253](https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.08253) (abstract-level)
- **PETS** (Chua, Calandra, McAllister, Levine; NeurIPS 2018) uses probabilistic ensembles with trajectory sampling and CEM-MPC, with no value function. It reports asymptotic performance close to model-free methods with "8 and 125 times fewer samples than Soft Actor Critic and Proximal Policy Optimization respectively on the half-cheetah task" — [arXiv 1805.12114](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.12114) (abstract-level)
- PETS-style planning without a terminal value at the same short horizon (H = 3) "performs poorly due to myopic reasoning" — [LOOP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.10066)
- **Horizon is the bottleneck for TD value learning (2025):** "biases (errors) in the target Q values accumulate over the horizon". In a combination-lock test, 1-step DQN had much larger Q errors than n-step DQN despite similar TD errors, and Q errors grew with distance to the goal; larger models and retuning did not fix it. Remedies are **value-horizon reduction (n-step)** and **policy-horizon reduction (hierarchy)**. n-step SAC+BC reached 88% on humanoidmaze-giant versus 44% for 1-step; SHARSA reached 94% on puzzle-4x5 versus 0% for SAC+BC. **Caveat:** the setting is offline goal-conditioned, with up to 1B transitions of scripted play data — [Park et al., "Horizon Reduction Makes RL Scalable", arXiv 2506.04168](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04168)
- **Action chunking for TD-RL (2025):** Q-chunking runs RL over chunks of h actions with a chunk-level critic, which gives an unbiased h-step backup and temporally coherent exploration. OGBench overall success was 86% versus 67% for RLPD; cube-quadruple 73% versus 0%. Chunk size h = 5 by default, h = 10 best on cube-triple, h = 50 failed. Limitation: it "may underperform where high-frequency feedback is essential". **Requires offline prior data** (offline-to-online, with a flow-matching behavior constraint) — [Li, Zhou, Levine, arXiv 2507.07969](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.07969)
- Temporally correlated exploration noise: pink (1/f) noise beats white and Ornstein–Uhlenbeck noise across many continuous-control tasks — [Eberhard et al., ICLR 2023, "Pink Noise Is All You Need" (OpenReview search; not on arXiv under that title)](https://openreview.net/search?term=Pink+Noise+Is+All+You+Need) (from prior knowledge, not re-fetched). iCEM uses colored noise in CEM-MPC — [Pinneri et al., CoRL 2020, arXiv 2008.06389](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.06389) (abstract-level)
- TempoRL learns when to repeat an action, which gives adaptive temporal abstraction over a flat action space — [Biedenkapp et al., ICML 2021, arXiv 2106.05262](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.05262) (abstract-level)

### Inferences
- **Horizon arithmetic for our setting:** γ = 0.99975 gives an effective horizon 1/(1−γ) = 4,000 steps (400 s), longer than the 1,800-step episode (γ^1800 ≈ 0.64), so the discount barely pulls the critic's horizon in. With 1-step TD, a tack payoff 20–80 decisions away must propagate through 20–80 biased bootstraps. This is exactly the regime where Park et al. show error accumulation. With 0.5 s blocks (h = 5), the tack is 4–16 macro-steps away; with heading setpoints it could be 1–3 high-level decisions.
- Applying TD-MPC2's discount heuristic to our post-repeat episode length (T = 1,800 at 10 Hz, or 360 with 0.5 s blocks) would give γ = 0.995 (capped) at 10 Hz. For reference, γ = 0.99975 at 10 Hz equals γ ≈ 0.99875 per 0.5 s block, so a block-level agent with γ ≈ 0.995–0.999 per block is a typical setting.
- Dreamer-style imagination (16 steps) at 10 Hz covers only 1.6 s, shorter than the tack payoff. Dreamer would therefore depend on its critic, just as our MPC depends on V, but its critic is bootstrapped on-policy in imagination and updated continually. Action repeat 5 (0.5 s) would make 16 imagined steps span 8 s, matching the horizon at which our true-model CEM always tacks.
- Since we have the **true simulator**, MBRL's main benefit (sample efficiency through a learned model) matters less to us than its **critic-learning recipe**: λ-returns or n-step returns, percentile return normalization, distributional or two-hot critics, and ensembles.

### Gaps
- MBPO's rollout-length schedule and PETS's CEM horizon were not re-verified; no specific numbers are given here.
- I found no published Dreamer or TD-MPC2 result on a task with γ ≥ 0.999 at 10 Hz and a sparse reward behind a 2–8 s non-monotone maneuver.
- DreamerV3 has no limitations section about sparse rewards; I found no reliable statement on its failure rate when the sparse event is rarely reached.

## Q3. Hierarchical RL, options and skill discovery without demonstrations: do they discover maneuvers flat RL misses, and which inject task knowledge?

### Takeaway
Hierarchy helps sparse long-horizon continuous control mainly through **temporally extended exploration and shorter credit-assignment chains**. The methods with the strongest demonstration-free evidence are the goal-conditioned ones (HIRO, HAC, Director) and the distance-aware skill discovery methods (LSD, METRA). Many rely on a **hand-chosen goal space** (HIRO, HAC: x-y position or a state subset), which is a form of injected task knowledge. Option-critic is the most knowledge-free but often learns degenerate options. RAPS, Puppeteer-style trackers and DMP action spaces inject the most structure: hand-designed primitives or a pretrained tracking controller.

### Cited Findings
- **Option-critic** (Bacon, Harb, Precup; AAAI 2017) learns intra-option policies and termination functions end-to-end with policy-gradient theorems, needing no subgoals or pseudo-rewards — [arXiv 1609.05140](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.05140) (abstract-level). Options tend to shrink to single steps; a deliberation cost was proposed to keep them extended — [Harb et al., "When Waiting Is Not an Option", AAAI 2018, arXiv 1709.04571](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.04571) (abstract-level)
- **HIRO** (Nachum, Gu, Lee, Levine; NeurIPS 2018) is off-policy two-level HRL in which the high level outputs goals (desired state changes) for a low-level goal-conditioned policy, with an off-policy correction that relabels high-level actions. It learned Ant Gather / Maze / Push / Fall behaviors "from only a few million samples". Goals live in the raw state space (a hand-chosen goal space) — [arXiv 1805.08296](https://arxiv.org/abs/1805.08296) (abstract-level)
- **HAC: Hierarchical Actor-Critic** (Levy, Konidaris, Platt, Saenko; ICLR 2019) trains up to 3 levels in parallel with hindsight action and hindsight goal transitions plus subgoal testing, and was claimed as the first to learn 3-level hierarchies in continuous state and action spaces. It assumes goal-reaching sparse rewards in a specified goal space — [arXiv 1712.00948](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.00948) (abstract-level)
- **HiTS** (Gürtler, Büchler, Martius; NeurIPS 2021) adds **timed subgoals** (state plus a time to reach it) for dynamic environments, where an untimed subgoal is ambiguous — [arXiv 2112.03100](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.03100) (from prior knowledge, not re-fetched)
- **Director** (Hafner, Lee, Fischer, Abbeel; NeurIPS 2022) builds a manager/worker hierarchy inside a learned world model from pixels. The manager selects **latent goals** and is rewarded for task and exploration; the worker learns to reach them. It "outperforms exploration methods" on sparse-reward tasks, including 3-D quadruped maze navigation, and avoids "manually specified goal spaces or subtasks" — [arXiv 2206.04114](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.04114). (A goal every K = 8 steps, discrete VAE goal codes and a goal-autoencoder exploration bonus are prior knowledge, not confirmed on the abstract page.)
- **DIAYN** (Eysenbach, Gupta, Ibarz, Levine; ICLR 2019) learns skills without reward by maximizing mutual information between skill and state under a maximum-entropy policy. The skills (e.g., running, jumping) can serve as primitives for hierarchical RL — [arXiv 1802.06070](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.06070) (abstract-level)
- **DADS** (Sharma, Gu, Levine, Kumar, Hausman; ICLR 2020) learns skills that are **predictable** under a learned skill-dynamics model and then uses MPC over skills for zero-shot goal navigation — [arXiv 1907.01657](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.01657) (abstract-level)
- **LSD** (Park et al., ICLR 2022) uses a Lipschitz constraint so that skills travel far ("dynamic") instead of making small state changes — [arXiv 2202.00914](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00914) (abstract-level)
- **METRA** (Park, Rybkin, Levine; ICLR 2024) covers a compact latent space tied to the state space by **temporal distance**. Mutual-information skill learning "can fail to explore due to a lack of incentives". It was billed as the first unsupervised method to find diverse locomotion in pixel-based Quadruped and Humanoid — [arXiv 2310.08887](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08887)
- **RAPS** (Dalal, Pathak, Salakhutdinov; NeurIPS 2021) uses a **manually specified** library of parameterized robot primitives whose arguments an RL policy chooses. On image-based sparse-terminal-reward tasks in three domains, it "substantially improves both the learning efficiency and task performance" independent of the RL algorithm, and beats methods that learn skills from offline expert data — [arXiv 2110.15360](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.15360)
- **Puppeteer** (Hansen et al., 2024–2025) is a hierarchical world model: "a high-level agent generates commands based on visual observations for a low-level agent to execute". Evaluated on 8 tasks with a 56-DoF humanoid; the abstract claims no "simplifying assumptions, reward design, or skill primitives" — [arXiv 2405.18418](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.18418). (That the low-level tracker is pretrained on mocap is prior knowledge, not confirmed on the abstract page; if true, it is demonstration-derived.)
- **Neural Dynamic Policies** (Bahl, Mukadam, Gupta, Pathak; NeurIPS 2020) embed dynamic-movement-primitive structure in the policy's output space, for RL and imitation — [arXiv 2012.02788](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.02788) (abstract-level)
- **Horizon reduction through hierarchy (2025):** Park et al. find that policy-horizon reduction (hierarchical subgoal policies) combined with value-horizon reduction gives the only non-trivial performance on all four of their hardest offline tasks (SHARSA). Offline data is required — [arXiv 2506.04168](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04168)

**Demonstration and hand-knowledge classification**
- Own experience only, no hand-designed skills: option-critic, DIAYN, DADS, LSD, METRA, Director, POLO, LOOP, TD-MPC2, DreamerV3.
- Own experience plus a hand-chosen goal space: HIRO, HAC, HiTS.
- Hand-designed primitives: RAPS, DMP- or NDP-structured outputs (structure only; parameters are learned).
- Needs offline or prior data: Q-chunking, SHARSA/HIQL (Horizon Reduction), and possibly the Puppeteer low level (unverified).

### Inferences
- Tacking resembles the "chain of actions" that flat exploration rarely strings together: a sustained rudder deflection through head-to-wind with a temporary speed loss before the gain. Hierarchy helps if a high-level action can represent "turn to the other board" as one decision. HIRO/HAC with a goal space of {heading, maybe speed} would do this. But **choosing heading as the goal space is task knowledge**, of the same kind as HIRO's choice of x-y position.
- Unsupervised skill discovery (LSD, METRA) on a sailboat would likely find "travel far in latent space" skills, i.e. sailing fast on various headings, because they reward reaching temporally distant states. Head-to-wind states are slow, so a tack reaches far in heading-space but not in position-space within a short skill window. Whether a tack emerges as a skill depends on the representation; I found no evidence either way for wind-driven vehicles.
- Option-critic's degeneration is a real risk at 10 Hz. Option termination at every step reproduces flat RL.
- The cheapest experiment consistent with this literature is a two-level setup with **timed** subgoals (HiTS) or a fixed-period manager (Director style, K ≈ 5–20 steps = 0.5–2 s), on our existing PPO/SAC stack, with intrinsic goal-reaching reward for the low level.

### Gaps
- I found no paper that applies HIRO, HAC, Director or METRA to sailing or to any wind- or current-driven vehicle with an obligatory non-monotone maneuver.
- Quantitative head-to-head numbers for HIRO, HAC, option-critic and DIAYN versus flat SAC/PPO were not retrieved this session; only abstract-level claims are given.
- Director's failure modes (e.g., underperforming flat Dreamer on some dense-reward tasks) were not verifiable from the abstract page.

## Q4. Heading/setpoint action spaces: does a higher-level action space change learnability in vehicle control, and what is the trade-off with minimal information?

### Takeaway
There is consistent evidence that the action parameterization strongly affects RL learnability and robustness: PD targets versus torques for characters, body-rates-plus-thrust versus rotor thrusts for quadrotors, and semantic maneuvers versus raw steering for driving. The effect is task-dependent and **not universally in favor of higher-level actions**. Setpoint actions shorten the decision horizon and smooth the optimization landscape, but they embed a hand-designed controller (task knowledge) and can cap achievable performance.

### Cited Findings
- **Characters:** Peng & van de Panne (SCA 2017) compared torques, muscle activations, target joint angles and target joint velocities. They conclude that "higher-level action parameterizations can significantly impact the learning, robustness, and quality" of policies — [arXiv 1611.01055](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.01055)
- **Quadrotors:** Kaufmann, Bauersfeld, Scaramuzza (ICRA 2022). A policy commanding **collective thrust and body rates** (tracked by a low-level controller) gives "more robust sim-to-real transfer" than one commanding individual rotor thrusts; the learned CTBR policy flew a real quadrotor at more than 45 km/h — [arXiv 2202.10796](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10796)
- **Generic continuous control (counter-evidence):** Schneider et al. (arXiv 2023) compared torque, velocity and position (PD) control with PPO on 6 tasks over 10 seeds. "No single action space is best across all tasks." Position and velocity control smoothed the loss landscape on Reacher, but torque was better on Walker-walk and on a joint-space Reacher variant. Controller gains matter — [arXiv 2309.06921](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06921)
- **Driving:** a driving-policy paper chose high-level actions ("change lane left", "accelerate at 2 m/s²") executed by low-level controllers, arguing that low-level control is less effective for high-level policies and that smaller action sets speed training — [arXiv 1907.05246](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.05246). A sim-to-real end-to-end driving study notes that a fully general continuous action space contains actions the maneuvers never need, which makes exploration harder and lengthens training — [arXiv 2012.07461](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.07461). Driver Dojo offers both raw control and semantic actions executed over many steps by a Stanley controller — [arXiv 2207.11432](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.11432) (search-snippet level)
- **Primitives as an action interface:** RAPS shows that changing the action interface to parameterized primitives improves efficiency regardless of the RL algorithm, at the cost of manual design — [arXiv 2110.15360](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.15360)
- A web search turned up no head-to-head study of setpoint versus actuator-rate actions specifically for vehicle control (design arguments and benchmark-level comparisons only) — [search summary; sources above]

### Inferences
- In our task, a heading-setpoint (or heading-relative-to-wind) action space tracked by a rudder controller turns a tack from about 20–80 low-level decisions with delayed payoff into a 1-decision change of setpoint sign. The reward for that decision arrives after the controller's turn. That collapses the credit-assignment chain, which Q2's horizon-reduction evidence says is the binding constraint for TD learning. It is very likely to make PPO/SAC discover tacking, but it **injects the concept of heading control**: it is a hand-designed primitive in the RAPS sense.
- Gradations of injected knowledge, least to most (our judgment, not from a source): (i) action repeat or chunking (0.5 s blocks, Q-chunking) with no semantics; (ii) rate-to-absolute change (target rudder angle or sheet length instead of rates), comparable to Peng's PD targets, little task knowledge; (iii) heading-setpoint tracking controller, vehicle knowledge but not tack knowledge; (iv) explicit "tack" or "gybe" primitive, task knowledge (RAPS-like). Option (ii) is a minimal-information change with good evidence (characters, quadrotors) that it helps, but the counter-evidence (Schneider et al.) means it must be tested.
- To keep "minimal information" as a claim, setpoint spaces can be reported as an ablation axis (flat rates → absolute angles → heading setpoint), with the low-level controller's design disclosed as injected knowledge.

### Gaps
- No sailing- or ship-specific RL study comparing heading-setpoint and actuator action spaces was retrieved; marine RL literature was not searched, to stay within scope and the call budget.
- Peng & van de Panne's quantitative results (which parameterization learned fastest) were not on the abstract page; the conclusion that PD targets learned fastest is prior knowledge and not verified here.
- Kaufmann et al.'s comparison numbers and their linear-velocity arm (if any) were not retrieved.
