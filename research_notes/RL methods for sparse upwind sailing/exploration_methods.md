# Exploration methods for discovering temporally extended maneuvers in sparse-reward, long-horizon continuous control (no demonstrations)

Scope note: the facts below come from primary papers (arXiv full text, read via PDF text extraction) unless a line says otherwise. "SailGym" in the Inferences means the setup the coordinator described: 10 Hz decisions, 1,800 steps per episode, 2-D continuous rate actions, a tack whose payoff arrives after 2–8 s (20–80 decisions), and a timeout local optimum at about −5.3 versus −10 for failure.

## 1. Temporally correlated exploration noise (pink/colored, OU, gSDE, lazy-MDPs, ez-greedy and descendants)

### Takeaway
Temporally correlated action noise is the cheapest intervention with the most direct evidence on tasks shaped like SailGym's. The canonical case is MountainCar with an action cost, which has a "do nothing" local optimum: there, the noise *type* alone decides between success and failure. Pink noise (β=1) is the best single default for off-policy SAC/MPO. For PPO the best default is β≈0.5, but tasks resembling MountainCar preferred β≥1.25. Persistence-based exploration (ez-greedy, TAAC, PolyRL) also helps on sparse tasks, but fails in environments where persistent random actions run into terminal "mines". Correlation alone has not been shown to solve tasks that need a precise *sequence* of different holds.

### Cited Findings
- **Pink noise (Eberhard, Hollenstein, Pinneri, Martius; ICLR 2023, spotlight/oral).** Colored noise has PSD ∝ f^−β: white noise is β=0, OU noise is related to red noise (β=2), and pink noise is β=1. Experiments used MPO and SAC and scored the mean normalized performance across benchmark tasks (shown include Cartpole-swingup, Hopper and Adroit Door). The authors' summary is "Pink noise works well on all environments we tested… Not true for white noise or OU noise!" Pink noise also beat β-schedules, random β selection and bandit β selection. The explanation comes from a 2-D "bounded integrator" (s_{t+1}=clip(s_t+a_t, ±c)): white noise explores best in small spaces, OU in large ones, and pink is robust across sizes in state-visitation entropy. Recommended default: `pip install pink-noise-rl` — [ICLR 2023 slides](https://iclr.cc/media/iclr-2023/Slides/12540.pdf); [ICLR 2023 page](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2023/oral/12540); [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=hQ9V5QN27eS)
- The follow-up paper restates the off-policy result: "Eberhard et al. (2023) found a common noise color, pink noise, to significantly outperform the previous default of uncorrelated white noise" — [Hollenstein, Martius, Piater, AAAI 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11091)
- **Colored noise in PPO (Hollenstein, Martius, Piater; AAAI 2024).** 16 environments, including Mountain Car, Cartpole Swingup, Ball-in-Cup, Hopper Hop, Door, UMaze/4-Rooms Point and Pendulum Swingup. β=0.5 was statistically comparable to the per-task best β* in 11/16 environments. White noise (β=0) was comparable in only 8/16 and pink (β=1) in only 7/16. The per-task best β was 1.25 for Mountain Car and 2.0 (red) for UMaze Point, and white noise lost significantly on both. With more parallel environments (larger update batches), more strongly correlated noise was better. The best setup was about 4 parallel envs (8,192 samples per update) with β≈0.5 — [arXiv 2312.11091](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11091)
- **Action noise study (Hollenstein et al., TMLR 11/2022).** Algorithms: DDPG, TD3, SAC and deterministic SAC. Tasks: Mountain-Car plus PyBullet Pendulum-Swingup, Reacher, Hopper, Walker2D and HalfCheetah. In Mountain-Car the action cost is quadratic and the goal pays 100, which "implies a local optimum: not performing any action and achieving a return of zero." Purely random Gaussian (σ=0.6) and OU (σ=0.5) policies got *similar* returns. Inside DDPG/TD3/SAC, however, "Learning either fails (Gaussian) or leads to success (Ornstein-Uhlenbeck)". Noise scale σ was the most important parameter. Decaying the noise over training generally improved performance and reduced seed variance. Larger state coverage often hurt on dense locomotion tasks — [arXiv 2206.03787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03787)
- **gSDE (Raffin, Kober, Stulp; CoRL 2021, PMLR 164).** Exploration noise is a function of policy features, and its parameters are resampled every n steps. n=1 gives unstructured noise and n = episode length gives the original SDE. On 4 PyBullet locomotion tasks (SAC, 1M steps, 10 seeds), gSDE-8 achieved "better performance with a lower continuity cost" than unstructured Gaussian noise. OU (σ=0.2) and parameter noise lowered the continuity cost but cost performance. If the sampling interval is too large, the agent "would not explore enough during long episodes" (shown for PPO on Walker2D). The method was also trained directly on a tendon-driven robot, a quadruped and an RC car. The evidence is all dense-reward: no sparse-reward test — [arXiv 2005.05719](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.05719)
- **ez-greedy (Dabney, Ostrovski, Barreto; ICLR 2021).** With probability ε, the agent repeats a random action for a duration n ~ zeta(μ=2) (heavy-tailed; z(n) ∝ n^−μ). Small domains: DeepSea, GridWorld, sparse MountainCar and sparse CartPole swing-up (linear function approximation). On Atari-57, R2D2 at 30B frames had a median human-normalized score of 16.44, R2D2+RND 11.14, R2D2+Bootstrap 16.62 and R2D2+ez-greedy 17.77. Human-gap was 0.102 / 0.151 / 0.096 / 0.077. Intrinsic-motivation methods "often (although not always) outperform z-greedy on the hard exploration tasks, but at a significant loss of performance on the rest." Stated failure modes: per-state randomized action effects, "GridWorld with many obstacles that immediately end the episode ('mines')", and mazes that change direction every few steps — [arXiv 2006.01782](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.01782)
- **ETGL-DDPG (Futuhi, Karimi, Gao, Müller; TMLR 05/2025).** Combines ϵt-greedy, a temporally extended ε-greedy that uses a light-weight search to generate exploratory options toward less-visited states (with a polynomial sample-complexity proof), with a dual replay buffer for rewarded transitions (GDRB) and "longest n-step" returns. It "outperforms DDPG, as well as other state-of-the-art methods, across all tested sparse-reward continuous environments" — [arXiv 2410.05225](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05225)
- **TAAC (Yu, Xu, Zhang; NeurIPS 2021).** Adds a closed-loop "act-or-repeat" binary policy on top of SAC, plus a compare-through Q operator. Evaluated on 14 continuous tasks, including 4 sparse-reward Fetch tasks and BipedalWalkerHardcore. Final normalized score 0.96 vs 0.78 for the second-best baseline; n-AUC 0.72 vs 0.55. Persistent exploration alone (TAAC-1td) scored 0.92 / 0.65, which already beat all baselines. Naive fixed repetition (SAC-Nrep) was the best baseline. A poorly formulated alternative (SAC-Hybrid) kept repeating with very high probability on hard-exploration tasks (Ant, FetchPush, FetchPickAndPlace) and scored very poorly — [arXiv 2104.06521](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06521)
- **PolyRL (Amin et al.; ICML 2021).** Locally self-avoiding, persistent exploratory trajectories, using radius of gyration as the spread measure. On SparseHopper/SparseHalfCheetah/SparseAnt with DDPG and SAC, it improved over uncorrelated noise, OU, parameter noise, FiGAR and OAC baselines in the reported plots — [arXiv 2012.13658](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.13658); [ICML 2021](https://icml.cc/virtual/2021/spotlight/9614)
- **Lazy-MDPs (Jacq, Ferret, Pietquin, Geist; 2022).** Adds a "lazy" action that defers to a default policy, and penalizes non-lazy actions so the agent focuses on critical decisions. The goal is *interpretability*, not exploration. Evaluated on Atari ("competitive performance… while only taking control in a limited subset of states") — [arXiv 2203.08542](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.08542)
- **Continuous-time view (Tallec, Blier, Ollivier; ICML 2019).** As δt→0, ε-greedy "is likely not to explore, i.e., to collapse to a deterministic policy". Consistent continuous-time exploration needs OU/Brownian-type processes whose trajectories converge as δt shrinks — [arXiv 1901.09732](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.09732)

### Inferences
- SailGym's timeout optimum has the same structure as the TMLR MountainCar case: the "do nothing" return is safer than the risky alternative. In that case noise type alone flipped SAC/TD3 from failure to success. That makes correlated noise the first, cheapest ablation.
- Arithmetic: with white Gaussian noise σ per step, the mean over a 50-step (5 s) window has std σ/√50 ≈ 0.14σ. A coherent multi-second rudder hold is therefore exponentially unlikely by chance. Pink or red noise puts power at periods comparable to the episode (1,800 steps), so multi-second holds are common.
- For PPO, the AAAI-2024 per-task optimum for MountainCar-like tasks (β=1.25) and for maze navigation (β=2) suggests trying β∈{1, 1.5, 2} for SailGym rather than the general default β=0.5.
- Correlated noise in *rate* actions integrates twice into heading (rate → rudder angle → yaw). OU/red noise on rates may therefore produce very large heading excursions and trigger the −10 failure. That is the ez-greedy "mines" failure mode, so the noise scale and color should be swept jointly. (This is my reasoning, not a tested result.)

### Gaps
- The exact per-environment numbers and environment count of the ICLR 2023 pink-noise paper could not be extracted: OpenReview and the MPI page blocked automated fetches. Only the slides and the follow-up's characterization were verified.
- I found no study of colored noise on a task that needs a *specific maneuver* (a turn through the wind followed by a hold), as opposed to MountainCar-style oscillation or coverage.
- The lazy-MDP venue (I believe AAMAS 2022) was not verified from the text, and the paper has no exploration results.

## 2. Action repeat / frame-skip / learned repetition; decision frequency; γ under repetition

### Takeaway
Theory and experiment agree that lowering the decision rate shortens the effective horizon, from 1/(1−γ) to 1/(1−γ^d). That speeds learning and exploration until the "price of inertia" outweighs it. Learned or closed-loop repetition (TempoRL, TAAC) gets most of the benefit without losing fine control. The principled γ for d-step repetition is γ_step^d, equivalently γ = γ_c^{δt} in continuous time (Tallec et al.). Q-chunking (NeurIPS 2025) is the newest evidence that chunked actions help long-horizon sparse tasks, but it assumes offline prior data.

### Cited Findings
- **Kalyanakrishnan et al. (arXiv 2021), "An Analysis of Frame-skipping in RL".** On Atari, RL "deliver[s] substantially better policies when run with d > 1—in fact with d even as high as 180". The paper defines a task-dependent "price of inertia" that upper-bounds the loss from action repetition, and shows this loss "may be offset by the gain brought to learning by a smaller task horizon". On Acrobot (5 Hz, Sarsa(λ)), d=3 was best and d up to 5 dominated d=1. The authors argue the key effect is horizon reduction "from 1/(1−γ) to 1/(1−γ^d)". Lowering γ to 0.99 without repetition helped (95.6 steps to goal, 11.8 better), but γ=1, d=5 "performs even better" — [arXiv 2102.03718](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.03718)
- **Tallec, Blier, Ollivier (ICML 2019).** DQN and DDPG "collapse with small time steps". "Q-learning does not exist in continuous time" because the advantage signal vanishes as δt→0. The paper proposes δt-dependent scalings γ_δt := γ^{δt} and r_δt = r·δt, plus an advantage-based reparameterization and learning-rate scaling, which give a δt-invariant algorithm — [arXiv 1901.09732](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.09732)
- **FiGAR (Sharma, Lakshminarayanan, Ravindran; ICLR 2017).** The policy outputs an action plus a repetition length. Reported gains on 31 Atari games (A3C), 5 MuJoCo tasks (TRPO) and TORCS (DDPG) — [arXiv 1702.06054](https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.06054)
- **TempoRL (Biedenkapp, Rajan, Hutter, Lindauer; ICML 2021).** Learns a skip-policy over how long to commit to an action, with skip-connection backups. Reported up to "an order of magnitude faster" learning than vanilla Q-learning (13.6× faster to reach reward 0.5 in a tabular domain). On Pendulum with DDPG, plain fixed action repetition slowed learning, while t-DDPG "only slightly lags behind vanilla DDPG" and roughly halved the number of decisions. Also tested with DQN on MountainCar and LunarLander — [arXiv 2106.05262](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.05262)
- **TAAC (NeurIPS 2021).** See Q1. Closed-loop act-or-repeat beat fixed-repeat, FiGAR-like, ez-greedy and hybrid baselines across 14 continuous tasks, with the gains attributed to persistent exploration — [arXiv 2104.06521](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06521)
- **Q-chunking (Li, Zhou, Levine; NeurIPS 2025).** TD learning over action chunks (e.g. 5 steps) gives "effective exploration with temporally coherent actions" and unbiased n-step backups. It reports the strongest aggregate success on 5 long-horizon sparse-reward OGBench manipulation domains. **Assumption:** it is "designed for the offline-to-online RL setting… leverag[ing] an offline prior dataset" — [arXiv 2507.07969](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.07969); [NeurIPS 2025](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/poster/117499)
- **Time limits (Pardo et al., ICML 2018).** When termination by timeout is part of the task, "a notion of the remaining time should be included as part of the agent's input to avoid violation of the Markov property". Otherwise state aliasing and invalid replay produce suboptimal policies — [arXiv 1712.00378](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.00378)

### Inferences
- γ under repetition in SailGym: with γ_step = 0.99975 at 10 Hz, the effective horizon is 1/(1−γ) = 4,000 steps (400 s). For 0.5 s blocks (d=5), keeping the same time-discounting means γ_decision = 0.99975^5 ≈ 0.99875, which gives 800 decisions (still 400 s). The episode drops from 1,800 to 360 decisions, and a tack payoff 2–8 s away drops from 20–80 to 4–16 decisions. Kalyanakrishnan's horizon argument applies directly.
- The planner that always finishes uses 0.5 s blocks. That is evidence the price of inertia at d=5 is small for this task, which is the condition under which frame-skip helps.
- Because timeout (−5.3) is a deliberate safe option, Pardo et al. imply the observation should include remaining time. Otherwise the critic aliases states near and far from the deadline.

### Gaps
- No paper found that isolates decision frequency as the factor enabling discovery of a *specific maneuver* in sparse continuous control, as opposed to learning speed on dense tasks.
- Braylan et al. (2015, "Frame skip is a powerful parameter") and Metelli et al. (ICML 2020, action persistence) were not fetched. They are only known here through citations.

## 3. Intrinsic motivation and novelty (counts/pseudo-counts, RND, ICM, VIME, state-entropy: APT, Proto-RL, MaxInfoRL)

### Takeaway
Bonus methods do solve classic low-dimensional sparse control (MountainCar, sparse CartPole swing-up, sparse HalfCheetah), and newer model-ensemble bonuses (MaxInfoRL, ICLR 2025) specifically fix SAC's failure under action costs and local optima. Controlled benchmarks, however, show that bonus methods often bring little over simpler exploration on hard tasks when the budget is held fixed, and that they hurt easy tasks. In a 2-D-state, low-dimensional task, simple counts or density bonuses are feasible. The open question is whether novelty in (x, y, heading, speed) points toward tacking or toward wandering.

### Cited Findings
- **VIME (Houthooft et al., NeurIPS 2016).** Information-gain bonus from a Bayesian dynamics model. Sparse versions of MountainCar, CartPoleSwingup and HalfCheetah give +1 only on escape, upright pole, or moving >5 units. TRPO+VIME "achieves significantly better performance than naïve exploration strategies" — [arXiv 1605.09674](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.09674)
- **#Exploration (Tang et al., NeurIPS 2017).** Hash-based counts achieve "near state-of-the-art performance on both continuous control tasks and Atari". Continuous tasks were sparse rllab MountainCar, CartPoleSwingup, SwimmerGather and HalfCheetah — [arXiv 1611.04717](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.04717)
- **MaxInfoRL (Sukhija, Coros, Krause, Abbeel, Sferrazza; ICLR 2025).** Adds an ensemble-dynamics information-gain bonus to SAC, REDQ, DrQ and DrQv2, with separately auto-tuned temperatures for entropy and information gain. The motivation is that existing methods "suffer in the presence of sparse rewards or local optima". Tasks include Pendulum, MountainCar and CartPole-swingup-sparse, plus action-cost variants (r_action = −K‖a‖²). "SAC struggles with action costs, especially in CartPole and Walker." MaxInfoSAC solved Pendulum swing-up "within 10K environment steps" and covered the state space much faster than SAC. Baselines: SAC, Disagreement, Curiosity, SAC-intrinsic, SAC-EIPO — [arXiv 2412.12098](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.12098); [ICLR 2025 proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/bd996108ed57d388866ca6deb7acf6cb-Abstract-Conference.html)
- **Bonus benchmarking (Taïga, Fedus, Machado, Courville; ICLR 2020).** "When the learning algorithm and sample complexity are kept fixed… little to no performance gain can be observed over older methods". Bonuses "typically hurt performance on the easier Atari 2600 games". Montezuma performance "does not correlate with performance on other hard exploration problems" — [arXiv 1908.02388](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02388)
- **URLB (Laskin et al., NeurIPS 2021 Datasets & Benchmarks).** Groups methods as knowledge-based (ICM, Disagreement, RND), data-based/entropy (APT, Proto-RL) and competence-based (DIAYN, SMM, APS). "No existing unsupervised RL algorithm can solve URLB" — [arXiv 2110.15191](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.15191)
- **APT (Liu & Abbeel, NeurIPS 2021).** Particle-based k-NN state-entropy maximization, used reward-free and then fine-tuned. On pixel DMControl it "dramatically improves performance on tasks that are extremely difficult to train from scratch". In the same experiments, a count-based bonus "fails to outperform train[ing] DrQ from scratch" because estimating high-dimensional density is hard — [arXiv 2103.04551](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.04551)
- In ez-greedy's Atari comparison, R2D2+RND *lowered* the median human-normalized score (11.14 vs 16.44 for R2D2) while improving some hard games — [arXiv 2006.01782](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.01782)

### Inferences
- In SailGym the state is low-dimensional, so the density-estimation problem that sank counts on pixels (APT) does not apply. Hash or grid counts over (position, heading) or an RND bonus are cheap.
- Risk: in a 40 m upwind task, novelty is maximized by sailing anywhere new, for example reaching across. Novelty is not specifically aligned with the tack. Bonus methods also change the optimal policy unless annealed. The MaxInfoRL action-cost result is the closest analogue to a "do nothing because acting costs" optimum.

### Gaps
- ICM and RND on low-dimensional continuous control with a deceptive timeout optimum: no primary result found.
- Proto-RL's paper was not fetched; it is covered only through the URLB categorization.

## 4. Go-Explore and continuous-control / robotics variants; robustification (backward algorithm)

### Takeaway
Go-Explore has the strongest record on deceptive sparse problems: it found reward in a sparse 9-joint robotic pick-and-place where PPO saw zero rewards in 1B frames. Its key assumption is the ability to *restore simulator state*, or else a goal-conditioned return policy. It then needs a robustification phase to turn brittle archived trajectories into a closed-loop policy. The backward algorithm (Salimans & Chen 2018) does this by resetting to states along the found trajectory, moving from the end toward the start. That cuts sparse-reward run time from exponential to quadratic in the gap between rewards. Newer variants remove handcrafted cells (Latent Go-Explore, ICML 2023) or use foundation models for "interestingness" (IGE, ICLR 2025).

### Cited Findings
- **Go-Explore (Ecoffet, Huizinga, Lehman, Stanley, Clune; Nature 2021).** Names two failure modes, "detachment" (losing track of frontier states) and "derailment" (exploration noise preventing a return to them). The method keeps an archive of cells, chooses a promising cell, *returns* to it ("by restoring simulator state or by running a goal-conditioned policy"), explores with random actions, and then robustifies. Robotics: a simulated arm with all 9 joints controlled picks up an object and places it in one of 4 shelves (two behind latched doors). Reward comes only for the target shelf. "PPO… does not encounter a single reward after training in this environment for a billion frames". Go-Explore solved it by restoring state, exploring randomly, and using a domain-knowledge cell mapping. Atari: superhuman trajectories on all 55 gym games. With domain knowledge, robustified Montezuma's Revenge averaged 1,731,645 and Pitfall 102,571 (max 112,000) — [arXiv 2004.12919](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12919); [Nature](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1038/s41586-020-03157-9)
- **Policy-based Go-Explore** (same paper). Restoring state "is not a requirement". A goal-conditioned PPO policy is trained to follow the best trajectory to the chosen cell, and the method "also performs self-imitation learning to extract as much information from these successful trajectories as possible", because successful obstacle crossings are "initially rare". Tested only on Montezuma and Pitfall with domain-knowledge cells, using about 1e10 frames — [arXiv 2004.12919](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12919)
- **Robustification and the backward algorithm (Salimans & Chen, arXiv 2018).** Every episode starts by resetting to a state from the trajectory. Start points begin near the end and move back once the success rate exceeds a threshold. In a toy model, standard RL run time "scales exponentially in the number of states between rewards" and the method reduces this "to quadratic scaling". Reached 74,500 on Montezuma from one trajectory. Go-Explore fed this algorithm 10 of its own exploration-phase trajectories (no human demonstrations), adding stochasticity for robustness — [arXiv 1812.03381](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.03381); [Go-Explore](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12919)
- **Latent Go-Explore (Gallouédec & Dellandréa; ICML 2023).** Replaces cells with a learned latent representation (forward dynamics, inverse dynamics or VQ-VAE) and density-based goal selection. It warns that "if the cell partitioning is not informative enough, Go-Explore can completely fail". In reward-free exploration on a continuous 2-D maze, a robotic arm with an object, Montezuma and Pitfall, it outperformed Go-Explore and other baselines — [arXiv 2208.14928](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.14928)
- **PBCS (Matheron, Perrin, Sigaud; 2020).** A Go-Explore-style method for continuous action spaces: a planning phase finds one trajectory, then a curriculum combines Backplay with skill chaining. "DDPG and TD3 are unable to steer a point mass in even small 2D mazes", while PBCS "greatly outperforms" them and Backplay without skill chaining — [arXiv 2004.11667](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.11667)
- **Intelligent Go-Explore (Lu, Hu, Clune; ICLR 2025).** Replaces the handcrafted heuristics with foundation models that judge interestingness, choose which state to return to, and choose actions. Aimed at foundation-model agents in text and game environments, not low-level continuous control — [arXiv 2405.15143](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15143)

### Inferences
- SailGym already runs a true-model planner over 8 s horizons, so cloning and restoring simulator state is evidently available. That is Go-Explore's main prerequisite. A cell of (x, y bins × heading bin × tack side) is easy domain knowledge. Exploring in 0.5 s blocks of random rudder/sheet holds would likely find gate-reaching tacks quickly, given the planner's success. The backward algorithm, or start states drawn from those trajectories, then gives the RL policy closed-loop robustness.
- This still counts as "no demonstrations": the trajectories are the agent's own. It does use privileged reset access, which is a stronger assumption than standard RL.

### Gaps
- No Go-Explore result on a low-level continuous-control task with a specified 10 Hz decision rate was found. The robotics experiment's control rate and frame counts per success were not extracted.
- No 2024–2026 continuous-control Go-Explore paper was found beyond LGE (2023) and IGE (2025, not continuous control).

## 5. Self-imitation learning and replaying the agent's own successes

### Takeaway
SIL (ICML 2018) and its variants exploit rare successes by imitating past (s, a) pairs whose return beat the current value estimate. The documented benefit grows as rewards become more delayed: the gap over PPO was larger on delayed-reward MuJoCo tasks. For sparse continuous control, transition-level SIL has been criticized, and episode- or trajectory-level variants (GASIL, ESIL, trajectory-conditioned SIL) were proposed. Go-Explore itself adds SIL because successful crossings are rare. No paper found directly measures the "forgetting rare successes" failure in SAC replay.

### Cited Findings
- **SIL (Oh, Guo, Singh, Lee; ICML 2018).** An off-policy loss imitates actions where R > V(s), derived from a lower bound on the optimal Q. A2C+SIL was competitive with count-based exploration (Reactor-PixelCNN) on hard Atari and improved the median over 49 games. On Montezuma, "A2C+SIL quickly learns to pick up the key as soon as the agent experiences it". PPO+SIL improved Swimmer, Walker2d and Ant. On delayed-reward MuJoCo (reward accumulated every 20 steps), "the gap between PPO+SIL and PPO is larger… compared to standard tasks" — [arXiv 1806.05635](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.05635)
- **GASIL (Guo, Oh, Singh, Lee; NeurIPS 2018).** Uses a GAIL discriminator on top-K past trajectories. "The optimal discriminator can provide intermediate rewards… even if the true reward… is delayed or sparse". It improved PPO on 2-D Point Mass and MuJoCo, "especially when rewards are delayed" — [arXiv 1812.00950](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.00950)
- **ESIL (Dai, Liu, Bharath; 2020).** Argues that transition-based SIL "performs poorly in handling continuous control environments with sparse rewards". Proposes episodic SIL with hindsight relabeling and a trajectory-selection module, evaluated on Fetch manipulation — [arXiv 2011.13467](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.13467)
- **SILCR (Chen et al., 2020)** (abstract only, via search). Adds self-imitation to SAC for sparse and delayed MuJoCo tasks and reportedly outperforms SAC on sparse rewards — [arXiv 2010.06962](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.06962)
- **ETGL-DDPG (TMLR 2025)** keeps a dedicated buffer for rewarded transitions (GDRB) and uses "longest n-step" returns "to more efficiently use the information provided by rewarded transitions" — [arXiv 2410.05225](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.05225)
- **Policy-based Go-Explore** uses SIL to retain rare obstacle-passing trajectories — [arXiv 2004.12919](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12919)

### Inferences
- In SailGym a finishing episode is +10 against about −5.3 for timeout. One lucky tack would give a large positive advantage for SIL, but 4M decisions under Gaussian noise apparently produced *no* finish. SIL therefore needs a discovery mechanism (Q1, Q2, Q4) in front of it. It is a retention mechanism, not a discovery one.
- A separate success buffer plus n-step or Monte-Carlo returns over successful episodes is the cheapest way to keep a rare tack from being washed out of a 1M-transition FIFO buffer. This is inference: no direct forgetting study was found.

### Gaps
- No primary study quantifies how quickly rare successes are forgotten in SAC/TD3 replay, or how much prioritized success replay prevents it.
- Diverse Trajectory-conditioned SIL (Guo et al., NeurIPS 2020) and Tang's generalized lower-bound SIL (NeurIPS 2020) were not fetched.

## 6. Curricula without demonstrations (reverse curriculum, GoalGAN, task-parameter curricula, HER)

### Takeaway
A reverse curriculum from the goal is the most directly applicable: it needs only arbitrary resets and one goal state, and it "funnels" policies backward from the goal. Applied to SailGym, the agent would start just below the gate and gradually further downwind. HER helps even with a single fixed goal but needs goal-conditioning. HER's authors also found that distance-shaped rewards broke both DDPG and DDPG+HER on manipulation. GoalGAN-style "goals of intermediate difficulty" give the selection rule (keep tasks with success in [R_min, R_max]). I found no evidence for task-parameter curricula such as rotating the goal direction.

### Cited Findings
- **Reverse Curriculum Generation (Florensa, Held, Wulfmeier, Zhang, Abbeel; CoRL 2017).** Assumption 1: "We can arbitrarily reset the agent into any start state". Assumption 2: at least one goal state is given, with no trajectories needed. Start states are grown from the goal by short "Brownian motion" rollouts (Gaussian actions) and kept as "good starts" when the policy's success there is intermediate. The process is described as sequentially composing locally stabilizing controllers, a tree "backwards from the goal state". Motivation: distance-shaped reward in ring-on-peg "leads to… plac[ing] the ring next to the peg", which is a deceptive optimum — [arXiv 1707.05300](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.05300)
- **GoalGAN (Florensa, Held, Geng, Abbeel; ICML 2018).** Trains on "Goals of Intermediate Difficulty", GOID = {g : R_min ≤ R_g(π) ≤ R_max}, with goals generated by a GAN trained on positive and negative examples. It explored a parameterized goal space more efficiently than SAGG-RIAC — [arXiv 1705.06366](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.06366)
- **HER (Andrychowicz et al., NeurIPS 2017).** Off-policy goal relabeling is "a form of implicit curriculum" and, unlike explicit curricula, "does not require having any control over the distribution of initial environment states". With a single fixed goal, "DDPG+HER performs much better than pure DDPG", but training on multiple goals learns faster even "if we care only about one". With shaped rewards r = λ|g−s|^p − |g−s′|^p, "neither DDPG, nor DDPG+HER was able to successfully solve any of the tasks" — [arXiv 1707.01495](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.01495)
- The backward algorithm (Q4) is the trajectory-based form of a reverse curriculum (exponential to quadratic run time) — [arXiv 1812.03381](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.03381)

### Inferences
- Reverse curriculum for SailGym: start inside the final approach (for example 5 m below the gate on either tack), then push the start downwind, which forces first one tack and then more. This needs only the resets the simulator already allows. The final task is the full 40 m start.
- Task-parameter curriculum (an inference, not tested): rotating the gate from a beam reach toward dead upwind first teaches close-hauled sailing, which needs no tack. Tacking becomes necessary only once the gate passes the no-go angle. That is a discontinuity, so the curriculum may stall exactly where the new skill is needed. A reverse-start curriculum avoids that cliff.
- HER's shaping failure is a caution about SailGym's distance shaping. Note, though, that SailGym's shaping is potential-based, while HER's λ=0 variant is not.

### Gaps
- No primary source found for curricula that rotate the goal direction or angle, or for ALP-GMM/PLR/CURROT-style task-parameter curricula applied to a maneuver-discovery problem.
- Numeric results for reverse curriculum (key insertion, ring-on-peg success rates) and GoalGAN (Ant maze coverage) were not extracted.

## 7. Surveys comparing families on hard-exploration continuous control

### Takeaway
The broad surveys (Amin et al. 2021; Ladosz et al. 2022, Information Fusion) catalogue these families: randomized/persistent action selection, bonuses, Go-Explore-style memory, and goal-based methods. Neither runs a head-to-head benchmark on hard-exploration continuous control. The closest controlled comparisons are narrower: Taïga et al. 2020 (bonuses, Atari), URLB 2021 (unsupervised DMC), TAAC 2021 (repetition and persistence baselines, 14 continuous tasks), and Hollenstein et al. 2022/2024 (noise type, scale and color).

### Cited Findings
- **Amin et al. (arXiv 2021), "A Survey of Exploration Methods in RL".** Covers ez-greedy as temporally extended ε-greedy, and PolyRL's correlated actions and locally self-avoiding walks. Notes that "perturbing parameters also works very well together with temporally coherent exploration", and that intermediate independent/episodic perturbation gave "faster learning and a way to control state space coverage and motor strain" — [arXiv 2109.00157](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.00157)
- **Ladosz, Weng, Kim, Oh (Information Fusion 2022), "Exploration in Deep RL: A Survey".** A taxonomy of reward-novel-state, diverse-behaviour, goal-based, probabilistic and hierarchical methods. Hollenstein et al. observe that action-noise schedules are "not mentioned in several recent surveys (Yang et al., 2022; Ladosz et al., 2022; Amin et al., 2021)" — [arXiv 2205.00824](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.00824); [arXiv 2206.03787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.03787)
- Controlled benchmarks: bonuses under fixed budgets ([Taïga et al. ICLR 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.02388)); unsupervised RL on DMC with no method solving the benchmark ([URLB](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.15191)); persistence and repetition methods on 14 continuous tasks ([TAAC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.06521)); noise color across 16 tasks ([AAAI 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.11091)).

### Inferences
- Across the evidence, a cost/assumption ladder suggests itself for SailGym. Cheapest, no new assumptions: colored/pink noise and action repeat d≈5 with γ=0.99975^5, then success-buffer or SIL retention. Next: count or information-gain bonuses. Most powerful but needing privileged resets: reverse-start curriculum, or Go-Explore plus backward-algorithm robustification. The last tier is closest to what makes the true-model planner succeed.

### Gaps
- No survey or benchmark found that compares all these families on the same sparse, deceptive continuous-control suite with a fixed budget. Results across papers use different algorithms, budgets and tasks and are not directly comparable.
- The 2023–2026 survey literature (for example Yang et al., IEEE TNNLS) was not fetched.
